11.1 Paracentesis

POCUS guidance reduces complications (bowel injury, bleeding) and is recommended by AASLD and SHM over landmark technique. LLQ is preferred (largest pocket, avoids midline vessels, avoids liver/spleen).

1
Identify ascites: anechoic fluid surrounding bowel loops. Confirm free fluid is present and non-loculated.
2
Entry site: LLQ, 15 cm from midline, lateral to rectus sheath. Avoid prior scars (adhesions) and inferior epigastric vessels (Color Doppler).
3
Measure fluid depth. ≥3 cm between peritoneum and bowel preferred. Mark with patient in same position.
4
Optional real-time guidance: sterile probe, watch needle advance through abdominal wall into fluid.
Inferior Epigastric Vessels

The inferior epigastric arteries run vertically within the rectus sheath. Puncture causes catastrophic abdominal wall hematoma. Use Color Doppler to identify before marking. Stay lateral to the rectus sheath or confirm vessel-free zone.

11.2 Lumbar Puncture — POCUS Assistance

POCUS identifies spinous processes, intervertebral spaces, and optimal needle insertion depth — particularly valuable when landmarks are impalpable (obesity, prior spine surgery, scoliosis).

  • Transverse view: spinous process as hyperechoic structure with posterior shadow
  • Sagittal view: interspaces visible as acoustic windows between spinous processes
  • Mark interspinous space and skin-to-subarachnoid depth
  • Average skin-to-ligamentum flavum depth: 4.5–6.5 cm in adults (deeper in obese)

17.1 Level 1 vs. Level 2 Competency
Level 1 — Supervised Performance
  • Scans personally performed AND interpreted by the learner
  • Direct supervision by credentialed attending
  • Image acquisition, pathology recognition, clinical integration
  • Forms the core of scan number requirements
Level 2 — Supervised Interpretation
  • Scans not necessarily performed by learner — may review archived cases, simulation, or computer-based modules
  • Interpretation, reporting, and integration skills
  • Supplements Level 1; expands case mix exposure

Other Reference Points Worth Knowing

FAST proficiency continues to improve well past initial competency, with one study citing a plateau around 79 exams.18 For LUS B-line quantification specifically, proficiency was reached after just 11 scanned zones in one learning-curve study.18 A large multicenter LUS study found trainees correctly classified 80% of lung regions after 25 supervised exams and 93% after 30.18 For gastric POCUS, Arzola et al. found 90% diagnostic success after an average of 24 scans, with a predictive model estimating 33 scans for 95% success.18 For FoCUS specifically, requirements vary enormously by society: the National Board of Echocardiography requires 150 exams performed and interpreted, the American College of Echocardiography requires 75 performed plus 150 interpreted, and the British Society of Echocardiography Level 1 accreditation requires 50–75 supervised exams plus a structured logbook covering specific pathology case-mix (minimum 5 cases each of reduced LV/RV function, 3 each of valve disease, 3 of hypovolemia, 2 of pericardial effusion, 1 of aortic root dilation, no more than 20 normal cases).18

Volume Is Not Competency

Scan numbers provide a floor, not a ceiling. Structured assessment tools (OSCEs, EPAs), deliberate practice with feedback, simulation, and portfolio documentation are equally important. Volume thresholds are minimum entry points, not certification criteria.18

ASA Diagnostic POCUS Certificate Program

The ASA offers a five-part Diagnostic POCUS Certificate of Completion: prerequisite evidence of prior training, online case-based interpretation modules, a supervised image-acquisition portfolio reviewed by a mentor, and a final 2-hour exam. The program offers up to 60 AMA PRA Category 1 Credits and counts toward ABA MOCA 2.0 Part 4 (up to 10 points).18 A separate ASA Gastric POCUS Certificate requires 30 logged mentored gastric scans plus a final exam, and can independently earn up to 10 AMA PRA Category 1 Credits and 5 MOCA QI Part 4 points.18

7.1 RUSH Exam — Rapid Ultrasound for Shock and Hypotension

The RUSH protocol was first introduced by Weingart et al. in 2006 and formalized as a structured clinical review by Perera, Mailhot, Riley, and Mandavia (EMCNA 2010).6 Mnemonic: HIMAP — Heart, IVC, Morrison’s, Aorta, Pneumothorax. A 2018 systematic review and meta-analysis pooling multiple validation studies found RUSH performs with pooled sensitivity 87% (95% CI 80–92%) and specificity 98% (95% CI 96–99%) for identifying the correct shock etiology, with AUC 0.98.7

7.2 Shock Pattern Recognition
Hypovolemic / Hemorrhagic
  • Heart: small, hyperdynamic LV, “kissing walls”
  • IVC: flat (<1.5 cm), >50% collapse
  • Abdomen: free fluid (RUQ/LUQ/pelvis)
  • Aorta: rule out AAA
  • Lung: normal (if no thoracic injury)
Cardiogenic
  • Heart: reduced EF, dilated LV, possible thrombus
  • IVC: plethoric (dilated, non-collapsing)
  • Lung: bilateral B-lines (pulmonary edema)
  • May see MR or wall motion abnormality (STEMI)
  • Abdomen: no free fluid (unless hepatic congestion)
Distributive (Septic)
  • Heart: hyperdynamic EF, normal or small LV
  • IVC: variable — often flat (relative hypovolemia)
  • Source: B-lines (pneumonia), consolidation, biliary, renal
  • Late septic shock: may develop septic cardiomyopathy (reduced EF)
Obstructive
  • Tamponade: Effusion + RA/RV collapse, plethoric IVC
  • Tension PTX: Absent sliding, plethoric IVC, small LV (impaired preload)
  • Massive PE: RV dilation, D-sign, McConnell’s, plethoric IVC
  • LV: small or normal, hyperdynamic (preload deprived)
7.3 Fluid Responsiveness

Fluid responsiveness = ≥10–15% increase in stroke volume after preload challenge. Only ~50% of hemodynamically unstable patients are fluid responsive — POCUS prevents harmful over-resuscitation.

7.4 SCCM 2024 Critical Care Ultrasonography — Evidence Summary

Díaz-Gómez JL, Sharif S, Ablordeppey E, et al. Executive Summary: Guidelines on Adult Critical Care Ultrasonography: Focused Update 2024. Crit Care Med. 2025;53(2):e441–e446.9 GRADE methodology across five clinical scenarios, developed by a 36-member interprofessional panel.

SCCM 2024 Recommendations
  • Septic shock: Suggest CCUS to improve clinical outcomes (Conditional, Low quality)
  • Acute dyspnea/respiratory failure: Suggest CCUS to aid diagnosis and guide management (Conditional, Low)
  • Cardiogenic shock: Suggest CCUS to guide management (Conditional, Low)
  • Volume management: Suggest CCUS for targeted management over usual care — mortality benefit noted (Conditional, Low)
  • Cardiac arrest: Insufficient data for or against CCUS over standard care (No recommendation)
Clinical Implications

All five recommendations are conditional, reflecting emerging but not yet high-quality evidence. The mortality benefit in volume management is the strongest finding. CCUS should be considered standard-of-care in hemodynamic assessment of sepsis, respiratory failure, and cardiogenic shock based on current evidence trajectory.

7.5 VExUS — Venous Excess Ultrasound

The VExUS grading system (Beaubien-Souligny et al., 2020)21 stages systemic venous congestion by combining IVC diameter with Doppler waveform grading of the hepatic, portal, and intrarenal veins. It extends the preload picture from IVC alone (see 7.3 Fluid Responsiveness) into a congestion-specific score — useful for distinguishing "needs more fluid" from "is already congested" in the shocked or post-resuscitation patient.

Hepatic Vein Doppler
  • Normal: triphasic, systolic (S) > diastolic (D)
  • Mild: blunted systolic wave, S < D but still antegrade
  • Severe: systolic flow reversal
Portal Vein Doppler
  • Normal: continuous, minimal pulsatility (PPF <30%)
  • Mild: pulsatile, PPF 30–50%
  • Severe: PPF >50%

PPF = (Vmax − Vmin) / Vmax × 100%

Intrarenal Vein Doppler
  • Normal: continuous venous flow
  • Mild/moderate: biphasic (discontinuous, two components/cycle)
  • Severe: monophasic (single short systolic spike/cycle)

Clinical Application

A plethoric IVC alone is nonspecific — VExUS grading helps decide whether a congested-looking IVC reflects true venous congestion that would worsen with further fluid. In the shocked or post-resuscitation patient with a positive fluid-responsiveness test (7.3) but a high VExUS grade, favor diuresis or fluid restriction over additional bolusing; reserve further fluid administration for VExUS 0–1 with a responsive study.

6.1 Abdominal Aortic Aneurysm (AAA)

AAA POCUS has near-100% sensitivity when performed by a trained provider. Any diameter ≥3 cm is aneurysmal. Repair threshold: ≥5.5 cm (men), ≥5.0 cm (women). Symptomatic or ruptured AAA requires emergent surgical consultation regardless of size.

1
Curvilinear probe, transverse scan from epigastric to umbilicus. Identify aorta left of midline, anterior to vertebral shadow. Marker to patient’s right.
2
Measure outer-wall-to-outer-wall diameter in transverse at widest point. Normal: ≤2.0 cm infrarenal; <3 cm = non-aneurysmal.
3
Identify key landmarks: Celiac axis (T12, first branch), SMA (L1, echogenic fat halo “mantle”), renal arteries (~L1-L2). Infrarenal aorta is the most common site for AAA.
4
Rotate to longitudinal. Look for intraluminal thrombus (hypoechoic crescent) and confirm measurement.
CRITICAL: Ruptured AAA

Do not delay surgery to characterize a ruptured AAA by CT. Hypotensive patient + back/flank pain + AAA on POCUS = vascular surgery NOW, OR immediately. Free fluid with AAA = ruptured until proven otherwise. The POCUS binary answer you need: is this >5 cm or not?

6.3 Renal & Bladder POCUS
Hydronephrosis
Anechoic dilation of collecting system. Graded: mild (pelvis only), moderate (calyceal), severe (cortical thinning). Bilateral: suspect BPH or retroperitoneal pathology.
Renal Stones
Hyperechoic focus with posterior shadow in collecting system. Twinkling artifact on Color Doppler increases sensitivity.
Cortical Echogenicity
Renal cortex isoechoic to slightly less than liver = normal. Increased echogenicity suggests medical renal disease (CKD, GN, diabetic nephropathy).
Bladder Volume
Volume = 0.52 × L × W × H. >300 mL = urinary retention. Normal post-void residual: <100 mL.

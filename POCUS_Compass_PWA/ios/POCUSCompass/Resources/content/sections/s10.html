10.1 Thoracentesis — POCUS Guidance

POCUS reduces PTX complication rate from ~4–8% (landmark) to <1% (POCUS-guided). Immediate pre-procedure marking strongly preferred over static marking done hours earlier.

1
Confirm effusion at posterior axillary line, 6th–9th ICS. Measure depth from skin to effusion and to lung/diaphragm.
2
Safe zone: effusion ≥10 mm depth. Avoid liver (right), spleen (left), diaphragm (inferior). Entry site one ICS above diaphragm at maximal effusion depth.
3
Mark entry site with patient in same position. Real-time guidance with sterile probe superior for small/complex effusions.
4
Insert needle just superior to rib (avoid neurovascular bundle under inferior rib edge).
5
Post-procedure: bilateral lung sliding confirms no PTX. Reassess if any respiratory change.
Diaphragm Injury Warning

Diaphragm rises significantly with expiration and in obese/supine patients. Always identify the diaphragm on POCUS before marking. Going below the diaphragm risks hepatic or splenic laceration. The safe triangle must be confirmed on imaging, not estimated anatomically.

10.2 Needle Decompression — POCUS Context

Needle decompression for tension PTX is a clinical diagnosis driven by hemodynamic instability. POCUS confirms absent sliding and guides the undifferentiated patient, but should not delay decompression when the diagnosis is clinically apparent.

  • Absent lung sliding (no B-lines) + hemodynamic instability = needle decompression now
  • Standard: 2nd ICS MCL or 4th–5th ICS anterior axillary line (preferred in obese)
  • POCUS post-decompression: sliding should return on affected side
  • If sliding does not return after needle: proceed to finger thoracostomy / chest tube
10.3 Pericardiocentesis — POCUS Guidance

POCUS-guided pericardiocentesis reduces complication rates to <5% vs. ~10% blind. Real-time guidance is now standard of care for non-emergent pericardiocentesis.

Access Route Selection
Subxiphoid (most common): SC4C view, anterior-to-posterior trajectory. Parasternal: PLAX view. Apical: A4C view. Choose largest, most accessible effusion pocket without intervening structures.
1
Identify largest effusion pocket. Measure skin-to-effusion depth. Subxiphoid approach: aim needle at left shoulder at 45°.
2
Advance under real-time POCUS or agitated saline confirmation (bubbles enter pericardial space, not ventricle).
3
Aspirate. If blood: agitated saline test confirms pericardial position (bubbles in pericardium vs. immediate chamber opacification = ventricular).
4
Post-drainage: RA/RV collapse should resolve. IVC plethora should improve. Reassess cardiac function.
Traumatic Arrest

Pericardiocentesis in traumatic cardiac arrest is temporizing only. Definitive treatment is surgical exploration and repair. Do not allow pericardiocentesis to delay resuscitative thoracotomy when indicated.

3.1 VTI & Cardiac Output — The Ultrasound Swan

LVOT VTI combined with LVOT diameter provides non-invasive stroke volume and cardiac output without invasive monitoring.

1
Obtain A5C (Apical 5-Chamber). Open LVOT between anterior MV leaflet and IVS.
2
Place PW Doppler sample volume 0.5–1 cm proximal to aortic valve in LVOT. Ensure beam parallel to flow.
3
Trace the systolic flow envelope. Machine calculates VTI (distance blood column travels per beat). Normal LVOT VTI: 18–22 cm.
4
Measure LVOT diameter in PLAX systole (inner-to-inner). Typically 1.8–2.2 cm. Square it (diameter constant across serial measurements).
5
SV = (π × [LVOT diameter/2]²) × VTI. CO = SV × HR. CI = CO / BSA.
Normal Values
  • VTI: 18–22 cm
  • SV: 60–100 mL
  • CO: 4–8 L/min
  • CI: 2.2–4.0 L/min/m²
VTI Fluid Challenge

≥10–15% increase in VTI after 100–250 mL bolus or passive leg raise predicts fluid responsiveness. Most validated dynamic POCUS parameter for fluid responsiveness.

Low VTI Pattern

VTI <15 cm + hypotension: consider low preload (hypovolemia), reduced contractility (cardiogenic), or obstructive physiology. Combine with EF, RV, and IVC to differentiate.

3.3 Pulmonary Embolism — POCUS Assessment

No single finding diagnoses PE. The constellation increases pre-test probability and guides urgency, particularly in the hemodynamically compromised patient.

RV Dilation (A4C)
RV:LV ≥1:1 end-diastole. Sensitivity ~50% all PE; >90% for massive PE with instability.
D-Sign (PSAX)
IVS flattening, D-shaped LV. Pressure overload pattern (systolic + diastolic flattening). Specific for RV pressure overload.
McConnell’s Sign (A4C)
RV free wall akinesis, preserved/hyperkinetic apex. Classic teaching: Sn 77%, Sp 94%.4 Current pooled evidence: Sn only 30%, Sp 98%.5 High specificity, poor sensitivity — a negative finding does not lower suspicion much.
60–60 Sign
RVSP <60 mmHg (by TRV) + pulmonary acceleration time <60 ms. Specificity ~94% for acute PE.
Thrombus in Transit
Highly mobile echogenic mass in RA/RV. Pathognomonic — emergent reperfusion indicated.
DVT on Compression US
Non-compressible femoral or popliteal vein. Increases PE probability substantially when RV strain present.
Massive PE in Cardiac Arrest

PEA with dilated hypokinetic RV + normal or hyperdynamic LV + no pericardial effusion on SC4C: strongly suggests massive PE. POCUS-guided thrombolytics or ECMO activation can be initiated when PE is the most likely diagnosis and CT is not feasible.

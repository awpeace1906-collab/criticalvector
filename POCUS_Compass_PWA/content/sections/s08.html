8.1 First Trimester — Emergency Assessment

Primary goal: Is there an intrauterine pregnancy (IUP)? A confirmed IUP essentially excludes ectopic pregnancy (except IVF patients with heterotopic pregnancy).

Pseudosac — Do Not Confuse with IUP

Ectopic pregnancy can produce a “pseudosac” — uterine fluid collection from decidual reaction. Pseudosac: centrally located, no yolk sac, no echogenic decidual ring. True GS: eccentric within endometrium, double decidual sign (two echogenic rings). Always confirm IUP by identifying yolk sac or fetal pole inside the uterus.

8.2 Fetal Heart Rate

Assessed via M-mode or Color Doppler. Normal FHR: 110–160 bpm. M-mode through fetal heart counts cycles per second × 60.

  • Bradycardia (<110 bpm): Fetal distress, maternal hypothermia, medications, heart block
  • Tachycardia (>160 bpm): Maternal fever/infection, fetal hypoxia, medications
  • Absent cardiac activity: Confirm in two orthogonal planes over 2 minutes before reporting fetal demise
8.3 Ectopic Pregnancy
Ruptured Ectopic — Lethal if Missed

Leading cause of maternal mortality. Any woman of reproductive age with vaginal bleeding, abdominal pain, and positive βhCG must have POCUS. Free pelvic fluid + absent IUP = ruptured ectopic until proven otherwise.

Empty Uterus
No GS, yolk sac, or fetal pole visible in uterus on transvaginal US with βhCG above discriminatory zone. High suspicion for ectopic.
Adnexal Mass
Extrauterine echogenic ring or complex mass adjacent to ovary. “Bagel sign” = tubal ring. Live ectopic: cardiac activity visible outside uterus.
Free Pelvic Fluid
Anechoic fluid in cul-de-sac (posterior to uterus). Positive βhCG + no IUP + free fluid: highly suspicious. Echogenic fluid = hemoperitoneum.
Free Abdominal Fluid (RUQ)
Free fluid in Morrison’s with ectopic = significant hemoperitoneum. Emergent surgical management required.
Transvaginal US: uterus (U), adnexal tubal ring/mass (red arrow). Empty uterine cavity with ectopic implantation in adnexa.

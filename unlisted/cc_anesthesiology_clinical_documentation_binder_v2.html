ASA Class

ASA Physical Status Classification

ASA I: Normal healthy patient.

ASA II: Mild systemic disease, no functional limitation.

ASA III: Severe systemic disease, functional limitation.

ASA IV: Severe systemic disease that is a constant threat to life.

ASA V: Moribund patient not expected to survive without the operation.

E modifier: Append for emergency surgery - delay in treatment would significantly increase threat to life or body part.

ASA class and the E modifier are billing-relevant qualifying circumstances (see Section 2) and must be documented on every anesthesia record, not inferred from the H&P alone.

Clinical Decision Tools & Perioperative Risk Scores

Risk Score - Cardiac

Revised Cardiac Risk Index (RCRI)

Estimates perioperative risk of major cardiac events (MI, cardiac arrest, pulmonary edema, complete heart block, ventricular fibrillation) for noncardiac surgery. 1 point each for:

- High-risk surgery (intraperitoneal, intrathoracic, or suprainguinal vascular)

- History of ischemic heart disease (prior MI, positive stress test, current chest pain from ischemia, nitrate use, or pathologic Q waves)

- History of congestive heart failure

- History of cerebrovascular disease (stroke or TIA)

- Diabetes mellitus requiring preoperative insulin

- Preoperative serum creatinine > 2.0 mg/dL (177 umol/L)

Approximate MACE risk: 0 factors ~0.4%; 1 factor ~1%; 2 factors ~2-7%; ≥ 3 factors ~5-11% (cited rates vary somewhat by cohort/source).

RCRI is simple and widely used but only moderately discriminating (AUC ~0.6-0.75 across validation studies) - the 2024 AHA/ACC perioperative guideline still references it as a starting point, combined with functional capacity and surgery-specific risk, not as a sole determinant of workup.

Risk Score - Airway

STOP-BANG (Obstructive Sleep Apnea Screening)

1 point each for:

- Snoring (loud, heard through closed door)

- Tiredness during the day despite adequate sleep

- Observed apnea (witnessed stopping breathing during sleep)

- Pressure (treated or untreated hypertension)

- BMI > 35

- Age > 50

- Neck circumference > 40 cm

- Gender male

Score 0-2: low risk of OSA. Score 3-4: intermediate risk. Score 5-8: high risk - consider additional perioperative precautions (postoperative monitoring, opioid-sparing analgesia, consider formal sleep study if elective and time permits).

Estimates risk of postoperative pulmonary complications after noncardiac surgery.

Score < 26: low risk. Score 26-44: intermediate risk. Score ≥ 45: high risk of postoperative pulmonary complications (respiratory failure, infection, effusion, atelectasis, pneumothorax, bronchospasm, aspiration pneumonitis).

Risk Score - PONV

Apfel Score (PONV Risk)

1 point each for: female sex, non-smoker, history of PONV or motion sickness, anticipated postoperative opioid use.

Approximate PONV risk by score: 0 factors ~10%; 1 factor ~20%; 2 factors ~40%; 3 factors ~60%; 4 factors ~80%.

Use this score to decide how many PONV prophylaxis agents to combine at induction - see the PONV Management Note in Section 5 for the prophylaxis-by-risk-tier approach.

Risk Score - VTE

Caprini Score (Venous Thromboembolism Risk)

A weighted checklist of over 40 risk factors (1-5 points each), commonly calculated via institutional order-set tool given its length rather than free-recalled at the bedside. High-yield categories:

- 1 point each (examples): age 41-60, minor surgery, BMI > 25, swollen legs, varicose veins, pregnancy/postpartum, history of unexplained stillbirth, oral contraceptives/HRT, sepsis (within 1 month), serious lung disease, abnormal pulmonary function, acute MI, CHF, history of inflammatory bowel disease, medical patient at bed rest.

- 2 points each (examples): age 61-74, arthroscopic surgery, major open surgery (> 45 min), laparoscopic surgery (> 45 min), malignancy, confined to bed > 72 hours, immobilizing cast, central venous access.

- 3 points each (examples): age ≥ 75, personal history of DVT/PE, family history of thrombosis, factor V Leiden or other thrombophilia, elevated homocysteine, heparin-induced thrombocytopenia.

- 5 points each (examples): stroke within 1 month, elective major lower extremity arthroplasty, hip/pelvis/leg fracture, acute spinal cord injury within 1 month, multiple trauma within 1 month.

Total score interpretation (general surgery population): 0 = very low risk; 1-2 = low risk; 3-4 = moderate risk; ≥ 5 = high risk - guides choice of mechanical vs. pharmacologic VTE prophylaxis and duration.

This is a summarized version - use your institution's validated Caprini calculator/order set for the actual documented score, since the full instrument has more granular, population-specific items than are practical to list here.

Intraop Record, Time Documentation & Attestations

The billing-critical section. Anesthesia time definitions, the CMS medical direction (TEFRA) seven-step attestation, and the modifier crosswalk. Verify against your institution's current billing/compliance guidance before use.

This section reflects general CMS medical direction (TEFRA) documentation requirements as commonly taught. Payer policy, state scope-of-practice law, and institutional compliance rules can vary and change. Confirm current requirements with your billing/compliance office before relying on this language.

Anesthesia Time Documentation

Time

Anesthesia Time Definitions

Anesthesia start time: The time anesthesia care begins in the operating room (or equivalent area), when the practitioner begins to prepare the patient for anesthesia. Not the time the patient enters the room if prep has not yet begun.

Anesthesia stop time: The time the patient may be safely placed under postoperative supervision (e.g., PACU nurse) and the anesthesia practitioner is no longer required to be in continuous attendance. Documented at handoff, not at skin closure.

Total anesthesia time is recorded in minutes on the anesthesia record and converted to time units per payer-specific increment (commonly 1 unit per 15 minutes; verify local convention) for billing.

Base units are assigned per CPT/ASA Relative Value Guide for the specific procedure and are separate from time units.

Qualifying Circumstances

Qualifying Circumstances (Add-On Billing Units)

Extreme age: Patient younger than 1 year or older than 70 years and one day at the time of the procedure (CPT 99100).

Total body hypothermia: Utilized as part of the anesthetic technique (CPT 99116).

Controlled hypotension: Utilized as part of the anesthetic technique (CPT 99135).

Emergency conditions: A situation in which delay in treatment would lead to significant increase in the threat to life or body part (CPT 99140; also append E modifier and document the emergent indication in the plan/MDM).

Documentation, not payment, is the reason to record these: traditional Medicare assigns CPT 99100-99140 a status indicator of 'B' (bundled) and does not pay them separately - their value is already folded into the base unit payment. Commercial payer policy varies and is trending the same direction (e.g., some large payers eliminated separate payment for these codes in late 2025). Document every applicable qualifying circumstance regardless, since it supports medical necessity and ASA physical status - but confirm with your billing office whether it is worth separately coding for a given payer.

Medical Direction Attestations (CMS/TEFRA)

Attestation - Medical Direction

Medical Direction of Qualified Non-Physician Anesthetist(s)

I am medically directing [1/2/3/4] concurrent anesthesia procedures involving a qualified non-physician anesthetist (CRNA/AA), consistent with CMS conditions for medical direction. For this case, I attest to the following:

1. I performed a pre-anesthetic examination and evaluation.

2. I prescribed the anesthesia plan.

3. I personally participated in the most demanding procedures of the anesthesia plan, including induction and emergence when applicable.

4. I ensured that any procedures in the plan I did not perform personally were performed by a qualified individual.

5. I monitored the course of anesthesia administration at frequent intervals.

6. I remained physically present and available for immediate diagnosis and treatment of emergencies.

7. I provided indicated post-anesthesia care.

I was not, during the periods described, performing another procedure on another patient that I could not immediately interrupt, nor was I unavailable for any other reason.

All seven conditions must be met and documented for the case to bill as medical direction (modifier QK/QY). If any condition is not met, the case must be billed as medical supervision (modifier AD) instead, at a different payment rate. Do not attest to medical direction language unless all seven steps genuinely occurred.

Attestation - Concurrency

Concurrency Statement

At the time of this case, I was medically directing [ ] concurrent procedures (maximum of 4 to qualify as medical direction). Case log/concurrency documentation attached or maintained per departmental policy.

Attestation - Medical Supervision

Medical Supervision (More Than 4 Concurrent Cases or Incomplete TEFRA Steps)

I was supervising [greater than 4] concurrent anesthesia procedures involving qualified non-physician anesthetists, and/or one or more of the seven medical direction conditions above was not fully met for this case. This case is billed as medical supervision (modifier AD), not medical direction.

Do not use medical direction attestation language (QK/QY) for a case billed under medical supervision (AD) - the two are not interchangeable and auditors specifically check for this mismatch.

Attestation - Personally Performed

Anesthesia Personally Performed

This anesthetic was personally performed by me from start to finish without medical direction of a non-physician anesthetist (modifier AA). No qualified non-physician anesthetist was medically directed during this case.

Spontaneous Breathing Trial & Extubation Readiness (ICU / Prolonged Ventilation)

Readiness Criteria

Screening Criteria to Attempt a Spontaneous Breathing Trial (SBT)

Consider an SBT once the following are met:

- Adequate oxygenation: PaO2/FiO2 > 150-200, FiO2 ≤ 0.4-0.5, PEEP ≤ 5-8 cm H2O

- Hemodynamic stability: no or minimal/low-dose vasopressor support, no active myocardial ischemia

- Adequate mentation: arousable (RASS -2 to 0), no ongoing sedation requirement, able to initiate a breath

- Afebrile, no significant uncorrected metabolic derangement

- Underlying cause of respiratory failure improved/improving

Meeting these criteria supports attempting an SBT; it does not by itself confirm extubation readiness - the SBT and RSBI below are what determine that.

SBT

Spontaneous Breathing Trial Note

Trial method: [T-piece / CPAP 5 cm H2O / low-level pressure support 5-8 cm H2O], duration [30-120] minutes.

Tolerance criteria (all should be met for a passed trial): respiratory rate < 35/min, SpO2 > 90%, heart rate < 140 or change < 20% from baseline, systolic BP 90-180 mmHg or change < 20% from baseline, no significant increase in work of breathing (accessory muscle use, thoracoabdominal paradox), no diaphoresis or agitation.

Outcome: [Passed - proceed to RSBI measurement and extubation readiness assessment below] / [Failed at ( time ) due to ( finding ) - returned to prior ventilator settings, reassess readiness and retry within 24 hours].

RSBI

Rapid Shallow Breathing Index (RSBI / Tobin Index)

Measured during the first 1 minute of unassisted spontaneous breathing (T-piece or minimal support), before fatigue sets in.

RSBI = Respiratory rate (breaths/min) / Tidal volume (L)

Respiratory rate: [ ] breaths/min - Tidal volume: [ ] L - RSBI = [ ]

Interpretation: RSBI < 105 predicts a good likelihood of successful extubation/weaning. RSBI ≥ 105 predicts a substantially higher likelihood of weaning failure.

RSBI is a single data point, not a standalone extubation order - integrate it with SBT tolerance, secretion burden, cough strength, and overall clinical trajectory. A good RSBI in a patient who cannot protect their airway or clear secretions is not a green light to extubate.

Cuff Leak

Cuff Leak Test (Post-Extubation Stridor Risk)

Indication: Prolonged intubation, traumatic intubation, large tube-to-airway size mismatch, or other risk factors for laryngeal edema.

Technique: Cuff deflated with the patient on assist-control ventilation, exhaled tidal volume measured with the cuff inflated and again with the cuff deflated (averaged over several breaths). Leak volume = inflated Vte minus deflated Vte.

Interpretation: A leak volume < 110-130 mL (or < 10-15% of the inflated tidal volume) suggests significant laryngeal edema and higher risk of post-extubation stridor/reintubation - consider [corticosteroids several hours prior to extubation, extubating over an airway exchange catheter, or delaying extubation].

An absent cuff leak does not mandate cancelling extubation on its own - it raises risk and should prompt a mitigation plan and a lower threshold for immediate reintubation readiness, particularly in patients who are otherwise borderline.

Sedation

ASA Continuum of Depth of Sedation (Reference)

Minimal sedation (anxiolysis): Responds normally to verbal stimulation. Cognition/coordination may be impaired. Airway, ventilation, cardiovascular function unaffected.

Moderate sedation: Purposeful response to verbal or light tactile stimulation. No airway intervention required. Adequate spontaneous ventilation. Cardiovascular function usually maintained.

Deep sedation: Purposeful response following repeated or painful stimulation. Airway intervention may be required. Spontaneous ventilation may be inadequate. Cardiovascular function usually maintained.

General anesthesia: Unarousable even with painful stimulus. Airway intervention often required. Spontaneous ventilation frequently inadequate. Cardiovascular function may be impaired.

Sedation is a continuum, not a fixed dose-response - document the level actually observed, since drug dose alone does not determine billing/documentation category.

Clinical Decision Tools & Perioperative Risk Scores

.revised_cardiac_risk_index

Revised Cardiac Risk Index (RCRI)

Estimates perioperative risk of major cardiac events (MI, cardiac arrest, pulmonary edema, complete heart block, ventricular fibrillation) for noncardiac surgery. 1 point each for:

- High-risk surgery (intraperitoneal, intrathoracic, or suprainguinal vascular)

- History of ischemic heart disease (prior MI, positive stress test, current chest pain from ischemia, nitrate use, or pathologic Q waves)

- History of congestive heart failure

- History of cerebrovascular disease (stroke or TIA)

- Diabetes mellitus requiring preoperative insulin

- Preoperative serum creatinine > 2.0 mg/dL (177 umol/L)

Approximate MACE risk: 0 factors ~0.4%; 1 factor ~1%; 2 factors ~2-7%; ≥ 3 factors ~5-11% (cited rates vary somewhat by cohort/source).

RCRI is simple and widely used but only moderately discriminating (AUC ~0.6-0.75 across validation studies) - the 2024 AHA/ACC perioperative guideline still references it as a starting point, combined with functional capacity and surgery-specific risk, not as a sole determinant of workup.

.stop_bang

STOP-BANG (Obstructive Sleep Apnea Screening)

1 point each for:

- Snoring (loud, heard through closed door)

- Tiredness during the day despite adequate sleep

- Observed apnea (witnessed stopping breathing during sleep)

- Pressure (treated or untreated hypertension)

- BMI > 35

- Age > 50

- Neck circumference > 40 cm

- Gender male

Score 0-2: low risk of OSA. Score 3-4: intermediate risk. Score 5-8: high risk - consider additional perioperative precautions (postoperative monitoring, opioid-sparing analgesia, consider formal sleep study if elective and time permits).

.ariscat_score

ARISCAT Score (Postoperative Pulmonary Complication Risk)

Estimates risk of postoperative pulmonary complications after noncardiac surgery.

.ariscat_interpretation

ARISCAT Interpretation

Score < 26: low risk. Score 26-44: intermediate risk. Score ≥ 45: high risk of postoperative pulmonary complications (respiratory failure, infection, effusion, atelectasis, pneumothorax, bronchospasm, aspiration pneumonitis).

.caprini_score

Caprini Score (Venous Thromboembolism Risk)

A weighted checklist of over 40 risk factors (1-5 points each), commonly calculated via institutional order-set tool given its length rather than free-recalled at the bedside. High-yield categories:

- 1 point each (examples): age 41-60, minor surgery, BMI > 25, swollen legs, varicose veins, pregnancy/postpartum, history of unexplained stillbirth, oral contraceptives/HRT, sepsis (within 1 month), serious lung disease, abnormal pulmonary function, acute MI, CHF, history of inflammatory bowel disease, medical patient at bed rest.

- 2 points each (examples): age 61-74, arthroscopic surgery, major open surgery (> 45 min), laparoscopic surgery (> 45 min), malignancy, confined to bed > 72 hours, immobilizing cast, central venous access.

- 3 points each (examples): age ≥ 75, personal history of DVT/PE, family history of thrombosis, factor V Leiden or other thrombophilia, elevated homocysteine, heparin-induced thrombocytopenia.

- 5 points each (examples): stroke within 1 month, elective major lower extremity arthroplasty, hip/pelvis/leg fracture, acute spinal cord injury within 1 month, multiple trauma within 1 month.

Total score interpretation (general surgery population): 0 = very low risk; 1-2 = low risk; 3-4 = moderate risk; ≥ 5 = high risk - guides choice of mechanical vs. pharmacologic VTE prophylaxis and duration.

This is a summarized version - use your institution's validated Caprini calculator/order set for the actual documented score, since the full instrument has more granular, population-specific items than are practical to list here.

.anesthesia_time_definitions

Anesthesia Time Definitions

Anesthesia start time: The time anesthesia care begins in the operating room (or equivalent area), when the practitioner begins to prepare the patient for anesthesia. Not the time the patient enters the room if prep has not yet begun.

Anesthesia stop time: The time the patient may be safely placed under postoperative supervision (e.g., PACU nurse) and the anesthesia practitioner is no longer required to be in continuous attendance. Documented at handoff, not at skin closure.

Total anesthesia time is recorded in minutes on the anesthesia record and converted to time units per payer-specific increment (commonly 1 unit per 15 minutes; verify local convention) for billing.

Base units are assigned per CPT/ASA Relative Value Guide for the specific procedure and are separate from time units.

.qualifying_circumstances

Qualifying Circumstances (Add-On Billing Units)

Extreme age: Patient younger than 1 year or older than 70 years and one day at the time of the procedure (CPT 99100).

Total body hypothermia: Utilized as part of the anesthetic technique (CPT 99116).

Controlled hypotension: Utilized as part of the anesthetic technique (CPT 99135).

.screening_criteria_attempt_spontaneous

Screening Criteria to Attempt a Spontaneous Breathing Trial (SBT)

Consider an SBT once the following are met:

- Adequate oxygenation: PaO2/FiO2 > 150-200, FiO2 ≤ 0.4-0.5, PEEP ≤ 5-8 cm H2O

- Hemodynamic stability: no or minimal/low-dose vasopressor support, no active myocardial ischemia

- Adequate mentation: arousable (RASS -2 to 0), no ongoing sedation requirement, able to initiate a breath

- Afebrile, no significant uncorrected metabolic derangement

- Underlying cause of respiratory failure improved/improving

Meeting these criteria supports attempting an SBT; it does not by itself confirm extubation readiness - the SBT and RSBI below are what determine that.

.asa_continuum_depth_sedation

ASA Continuum of Depth of Sedation (Reference)

Minimal sedation (anxiolysis): Responds normally to verbal stimulation. Cognition/coordination may be impaired. Airway, ventilation, cardiovascular function unaffected.

Moderate sedation: Purposeful response to verbal or light tactile stimulation. No airway intervention required. Adequate spontaneous ventilation. Cardiovascular function usually maintained.

Deep sedation: Purposeful response following repeated or painful stimulation. Airway intervention may be required. Spontaneous ventilation may be inadequate. Cardiovascular function usually maintained.

General anesthesia: Unarousable even with painful stimulus. Airway intervention often required. Spontaneous ventilation frequently inadequate. Cardiovascular function may be impaired.

Sedation is a continuum, not a fixed dose-response - document the level actually observed, since drug dose alone does not determine billing/documentation category.

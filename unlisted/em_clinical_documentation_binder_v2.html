Review of Systems (ED, 10-Point)

ROS - General ED

Review of Systems

Constitutional: Denies fever, chills, night sweats, unintentional weight loss.

HEENT: Denies headache, vision changes, ear pain, sore throat, neck stiffness.

Cardiovascular: Denies chest pain, palpitations, syncope, orthopnea, leg swelling.

Respiratory: Denies shortness of breath, cough, hemoptysis, pleuritic chest pain.

Gastrointestinal: Denies abdominal pain, nausea, vomiting, hematemesis, melena, hematochezia.

Genitourinary: Denies dysuria, hematuria, flank pain, vaginal bleeding, testicular pain.

Musculoskeletal: Denies trauma, focal weakness, joint swelling, back pain.

Neurological: Denies headache, dizziness, focal weakness, numbness, slurred speech, seizure, loss of consciousness.

Psychiatric: Denies suicidal or homicidal ideation, hallucinations.

10-point ROS reviewed and otherwise negative except as noted in the HPI.

ROS - Short

ROS Short

10-point ROS reviewed and otherwise negative except as noted in the HPI.

One-line version, for charts where the full 10-point system list isn't needed and you just want the closing attestation on its own.

ROS Add-On

Focused ROS Add-On: Back Pain

Denies weakness, numbness, or tingling in the legs; loss of bowel or bladder control; saddle anesthesia; fevers; chronic steroid use; IV drug use; history of malignancy; prior back surgery.

Use to document the red-flag negatives specific to a back pain visit, on top of the general ROS above.

Clinical Decision Tools

CDR - Head Injury

Canadian CT Head Rule

Applies to: blunt head trauma within the past 24 hours with loss of consciousness, definite amnesia, or witnessed disorientation, and GCS 13-15 at assessment. Age > 16.

High-risk criteria (for neurologic intervention - CT indicated if any present):

- GCS < 15 at 2 hours post-injury

- Suspected open or depressed skull fracture

- Any sign of basilar skull fracture (hemotympanum, raccoon eyes, CSF oto/rhinorrhea, Battle's sign)

- 2 or more episodes of vomiting

- Age 65 years or older

Medium-risk criteria (for brain injury detection on CT - CT indicated if any present):

- Retrograde amnesia to the event of 30 minutes or more

- Dangerous mechanism (pedestrian struck, occupant ejected from vehicle, fall from height ≥ 3 feet / 5 stairs)

Patient did not meet Canadian CT Head Rule criteria based on the above, so CT was not performed to further rule out acute intracranial pathology. / Patient met the following criteria, and CT was performed: [ ]

Local modification: patients on anticoagulation/antiplatelet therapy or with a seizure after injury are treated as automatic CT indications at this institution regardless of rule score - the original derivation and validation cohorts did not include these patients. Confirm this modification reflects your actual departmental practice before relying on it, since it is a local convention, not part of the validated rule itself.

CDR - Pediatric Head Injury

PECARN Pediatric Head Injury Algorithm

Applies to: blunt head trauma, GCS 14-15, presenting within 24 hours. Two age-stratified branches (age < 2 years and age ≥ 2 years) - this is a summary; consult the full decision tree for borderline combinations.

Age < 2 years - CT recommended if: GCS < 15, altered mental status, or palpable skull fracture.

- Intermediate risk (CT vs. observation, shared decision-making): occipital/parietal/temporal scalp hematoma, history of LOC ≥ 5 sec, severe mechanism, not acting normally per parent/caregiver.

Age ≥ 2 years - CT recommended if: GCS < 15, altered mental status, or signs of basilar skull fracture.

- Intermediate risk (CT vs. observation, shared decision-making): any history of LOC, history of vomiting, severe mechanism, severe headache.

If none of the above present in either age group: CT not recommended - very low risk of clinically important traumatic brain injury.

Rule applies only to GCS 14-15; unlike the Canadian CT Head Rule, PECARN explicitly supports an observation period (typically 4-6 hours) as an alternative to immediate CT for intermediate-risk patients rather than treating intermediate risk as an automatic imaging trigger.

Clinical Decision Tools - Chest Pain & PE/DVT

CDR - Chest Pain

HEART Score

Used for risk-stratifying ED chest pain patients for 6-week major adverse cardiac events (MACE).

Category 0 points 1 point 2 points
History Slightly suspicious Moderately suspicious Highly suspicious
EKG Normal Non-specific repolarization disturbance Significant ST deviation
Age < 45 45-64 ≥ 65
Risk factors* None 1-2 risk factors ≥ 3 risk factors, or known atherosclerotic disease
Troponin ≤ normal limit 1-3x normal limit > 3x normal limit

HEART Score Interpretation

*Risk factors: hypertension, hypercholesterolemia, diabetes, obesity (BMI > 30), smoking (current or in past 3 months), family history of atherosclerotic disease, known atherosclerotic disease (prior MI/PCI/CABG, CVA/TIA, peripheral arterial disease).

Score 0-3 (low risk): MACE risk approximately 0.9-1.7% - consider discharge with outpatient follow-up.

Score 4-6 (moderate risk): MACE risk approximately 12-16.6% - consider observation, serial troponins, stress testing or further workup.

Score 7-10 (high risk): MACE risk approximately 50-65% - admission and early cardiology involvement generally warranted.

CDR - PE

PERC Rule (Pulmonary Embolism Rule-out Criteria)

Use only in patients already judged low pretest probability for PE (gestalt or Wells < 2) - PERC is a rule-out tool, not a screening tool. All 8 criteria must be negative to consider PE ruled out without further testing.

- Age < 50

- Heart rate < 100

- SpO2 ≥ 95% on room air

- No hemoptysis

- No estrogen use

- No prior DVT/PE

- No unilateral leg swelling

- No surgery or trauma requiring hospitalization within the prior 4 weeks

If pretest probability is anything other than low, PERC does not apply - proceed to Wells criteria and/or D-dimer/imaging instead.

CDR - PE

Wells' Criteria for Pulmonary Embolism

Dichotomized version (favored by current guidelines, used with age-adjusted D-dimer):

Criterion Points
Clinical signs/symptoms of DVT (leg swelling, pain with palpation) 3
PE is the #1 diagnosis, or equally likely 3
Heart rate > 100 1.5
Immobilization ≥ 3 days or surgery in the previous 4 weeks 1.5
Previous, objectively diagnosed DVT/PE 1.5
Hemoptysis 1
Malignancy (treatment within 6 months, or palliative) 1

Wells PE Interpretation

Dichotomized: ≤ 4 points = "PE unlikely" (consider high-sensitivity D-dimer, PERC if also low-risk); > 4 points = "PE likely" (consider CTPA, D-dimer not recommended to rule out).

Three-tier (original): < 2 = low risk (~1.3% PE prevalence); 2-6 = moderate risk (~16.2%); > 6 = high risk (~37.5%).

CDR - DVT

Wells' Criteria for Deep Vein Thrombosis

A distinct scoring system from Wells' PE criteria - do not confuse the two.

Criterion Points
Active cancer (treatment within 6 months, or palliative) 1
Paralysis, paresis, or recent plaster immobilization of the lower extremity 1
Recently bedridden > 3 days, or major surgery within 12 weeks 1
Localized tenderness along the deep venous system 1
Entire leg swollen 1
Calf swelling > 3 cm compared to the asymptomatic leg (measured 10 cm below tibial tuberosity) 1
Pitting edema confined to the symptomatic leg 1
Collateral superficial veins (non-varicose) 1
Previously documented DVT 1
Alternative diagnosis at least as likely as DVT -2

Wells DVT Interpretation

Two-tier: ≥ 2 = DVT likely (proceed to ultrasound); < 2 = DVT unlikely (consider D-dimer first).

Three-tier (original): 0 = low risk (~5% prevalence); 1-2 = moderate risk (~17%); ≥ 3 = high risk (~53%).

Clinical Decision Tools - Trauma & MSK

CDR - C-Spine

NEXUS Criteria

C-spine imaging may be safely omitted if ALL of the following are absent:

- Posterior midline cervical tenderness

- Evidence of intoxication

- Abnormal level of alertness

- Focal neurologic deficit

- Painful distracting injury

CDR - C-Spine

Canadian C-Spine Rule

For alert (GCS 15), hemodynamically stable trauma patients where cervical spine injury is a concern.

Step 1 - Any high-risk factor mandates imaging:

- Age ≥ 65

- Dangerous mechanism (fall from ≥ 3 feet/5 stairs, axial load to head, high-speed MVC > 100 km/h, rollover, ejection, motorized recreational vehicle collision, bicycle collision)

- Paresthesias in the extremities

Step 2 - Any low-risk factor allows safe assessment of range of motion:

- Simple rear-end motor vehicle collision

- Sitting position in the ED

- Ambulatory at any time since the injury

- Delayed onset of neck pain

- Absence of midline C-spine tenderness

Step 3: If a low-risk factor is present, patient is able to actively rotate the neck 45 degrees left and right - if yes, no imaging needed; if unable, or if no low-risk factor is present, imaging is required.

NEXUS and the Canadian C-Spine Rule are alternative, not complementary, tools - most departments pick one as their default rather than applying both to the same patient.

CDR - MSK

Ottawa Ankle Rule

Ankle X-ray series indicated if pain in the malleolar zone AND any of:

- Bone tenderness at the posterior edge or tip of the lateral malleolus (distal 6 cm)

- Bone tenderness at the posterior edge or tip of the medial malleolus (distal 6 cm)

- Inability to bear weight both immediately after the injury and in the ED (4 steps)

Foot X-ray series indicated if pain in the midfoot zone AND any of:

- Bone tenderness at the base of the 5th metatarsal

- Bone tenderness at the navicular

- Inability to bear weight both immediately after the injury and in the ED (4 steps)

CDR - MSK

Ottawa Knee Rule

Knee X-ray indicated if any of the following present:

- Age ≥ 55

- Isolated tenderness of the patella (no other bone tenderness)

- Tenderness at the head of the fibula

- Inability to flex the knee to 90 degrees

- Inability to bear weight both immediately after the injury and in the ED (4 steps), regardless of limping

Clinical Decision Tools - Medical & Infectious

CDR - Sepsis

qSOFA (Quick Sequential Organ Failure Assessment)

Bedside sepsis screening tool - 1 point each for:

- Respiratory rate ≥ 22/min

- Altered mentation (GCS < 15)

- Systolic blood pressure ≤ 100 mmHg

Score ≥ 2 is associated with increased risk of poor outcome and should prompt further sepsis workup and consideration of the full SOFA score/source control - qSOFA is a screening tool, not a diagnostic criterion for sepsis itself.

CDR - Pneumonia

CURB-65

Pneumonia severity/disposition tool - 1 point each for:

- Confusion (new onset)

- Urea > 7 mmol/L (BUN > 19 mg/dL)

- Respiratory rate ≥ 30

- Blood pressure (systolic < 90 or diastolic ≤ 60)

- Age ≥ 65

Score 0-1: low risk, outpatient treatment reasonable. Score 2: consider short inpatient stay or closely supervised outpatient treatment. Score ≥ 3: severe pneumonia, admit; consider ICU if score ≥ 4.

CRB-65 (omitting urea) can be used when a BUN is not readily available, e.g. in outpatient/primary care settings.

CDR - Pharyngitis

Centor / McIsaac Criteria

Estimates likelihood of Group A Streptococcal pharyngitis - 1 point each for:

- Tonsillar exudate

- Tender anterior cervical lymphadenopathy

- Fever by history

- Absence of cough

McIsaac age adjustment: age 3-14, add 1 point; age 15-44, no adjustment; age ≥ 45, subtract 1 point.

Score ≤ 1: no testing or antibiotics. Score 2-3: rapid strep testing/culture, treat if positive. Score ≥ 4: consider empiric treatment, though testing is still reasonable.

CDR - GI Bleed

Glasgow-Blatchford Score

Risk-stratifies upper GI bleed for need for intervention (transfusion, endoscopy, surgery) - a score of 0 supports safe outpatient management.

Parameter Value Points
BUN (mg/dL) < 18.2 / 18.2-22.4 / 22.4-28 / 28-70 / ≥ 70 0 / 2 / 3 / 4 / 6
Hemoglobin, men (g/dL) ≥ 13 / 12-13 / 10-12 / < 10 0 / 1 / 3 / 6
Hemoglobin, women (g/dL) ≥ 12 / 10-12 / < 10 0 / 1 / 6
Systolic BP (mmHg) ≥ 110 / 100-109 / 90-99 / < 90 0 / 1 / 2 / 3
Other findings Pulse ≥ 100 / Melena / Syncope / Hepatic disease / Cardiac failure 1 / 1 / 2 / 2 / 2 (each if present)
CDR - Stroke/TIA

ABCD2 Score

Estimates 2-day stroke risk after TIA.

- Age ≥ 60: 1 point

- Blood pressure ≥ 140/90 at evaluation: 1 point

- Clinical features: unilateral weakness (2 points), speech disturbance without weakness (1 point), other (0 points)

- Duration: ≥ 60 min (2 points), 10-59 min (1 point), < 10 min (0 points)

- Diabetes: 1 point

Score 0-3: low risk (2-day stroke risk ~1%). Score 4-5: moderate risk (~4.1%). Score 6-7: high risk (~8.1%) - generally warrants admission/expedited workup.

ABCD2 has known limitations (does not incorporate imaging findings or atrial fibrillation) - many centers now use it alongside, not instead of, expedited TIA imaging pathways.

CDR - Abdominal Pain

Alvarado Score

Estimates likelihood of acute appendicitis.

- Migration of pain to RLQ: 1

- Anorexia: 1

- Nausea/vomiting: 1

- Tenderness in RLQ: 2

- Rebound pain: 1

- Elevated temperature (> 37.3 C / 99.1 F): 1

- Leukocytosis (> 10,000): 2

- Left shift (neutrophilia): 1

Total 10 points. Score ≤ 4: appendicitis unlikely. Score 5-6: possible, further imaging warranted. Score 7-8: probable. Score 9-10: highly probable, surgical consultation.

CDR - Syncope

San Francisco Syncope Rule (CHESS)

Screens for risk of serious outcome after syncope - any one positive criterion warrants further workup/observation:

- CHF history

- Hematocrit < 30%

- EKG abnormal (new changes or non-sinus rhythm)

- Shortness of breath

- Systolic BP < 90 mmHg at triage

Compare against the reassuring/discharge features already listed under the Syncope discharge instruction in Section 4 - a negative CHESS screen supports, but does not replace, overall clinical gestalt and shared decision-making.

Documentation Habit

Charting to Support the MDM Level Billed

Number/complexity of problems: name each problem actively managed this visit, not just the chief complaint - a chronic illness with exacerbation, or multiple stable chronic illnesses being actively considered in the differential, both count.

Data reviewed: explicitly document review of old records, discussion with another treating provider (by phone, in person, or via the chart), and independent interpretation of imaging/EKG obtained elsewhere (not just the official read) - these are easy to do but easy to forget to write down.

Risk: state the risk explicitly in the MDM - prescription drug management, the specific procedure's risk, or the reasoning behind a disposition decision (admit vs. discharge, resuscitate vs. comfort care) - risk is often present in the chart implicitly but needs to be named to count.

Brief Resolved Unexplained Event (BRUE)

A brief resolved unexplained event (BRUE) happens suddenly and can be frightening. It is a diagnosis given after your baby's doctor has examined your baby and found no known concerning cause for the event. During a BRUE, a baby may seem to stop breathing, change skin color, tense or go limp, or seem to pass out, then recover on their own within a short time.

Lower-risk features: older than 60 days; born on time; did not need CPR; the event lasted less than 1 minute; this was the only such event.

Common questions: A BRUE does not increase the risk of SIDS, though a safe sleep environment is always important (see healthychildren.org/safesleep). Babies at lower risk do not need to stay in the hospital, do not need blood tests or X-ray imaging, and do not need home cardiac or respiratory monitoring. No special care is needed afterward - continue to care for your baby normally.

If it happens again: call 911 if it feels life-threatening; otherwise call your baby's doctor. It's a good idea for every caregiver to learn infant CPR.

Adapted from AAP patient education material, 2016.

.pecarn_pediatric_head_injury

PECARN Pediatric Head Injury Algorithm

Applies to: blunt head trauma, GCS 14-15, presenting within 24 hours. Two age-stratified branches (age < 2 years and age ≥ 2 years) - this is a summary; consult the full decision tree for borderline combinations.

Age < 2 years - CT recommended if: GCS < 15, altered mental status, or palpable skull fracture.

- Intermediate risk (CT vs. observation, shared decision-making): occipital/parietal/temporal scalp hematoma, history of LOC ≥ 5 sec, severe mechanism, not acting normally per parent/caregiver.

Age ≥ 2 years - CT recommended if: GCS < 15, altered mental status, or signs of basilar skull fracture.

- Intermediate risk (CT vs. observation, shared decision-making): any history of LOC, history of vomiting, severe mechanism, severe headache.

If none of the above present in either age group: CT not recommended - very low risk of clinically important traumatic brain injury.

Rule applies only to GCS 14-15; unlike the Canadian CT Head Rule, PECARN explicitly supports an observation period (typically 4-6 hours) as an alternative to immediate CT for intermediate-risk patients rather than treating intermediate risk as an automatic imaging trigger.

Canadian C-Spine Rule

For alert (GCS 15), hemodynamically stable trauma patients where cervical spine injury is a concern.

Step 1 - Any high-risk factor mandates imaging:

- Age ≥ 65

- Dangerous mechanism (fall from ≥ 3 feet/5 stairs, axial load to head, high-speed MVC > 100 km/h, rollover, ejection, motorized recreational vehicle collision, bicycle collision)

- Paresthesias in the extremities

- Simple rear-end motor vehicle collision

- Sitting position in the ED

- Ambulatory at any time since the injury

- Delayed onset of neck pain

- Absence of midline C-spine tenderness

.qsofa

Bedside sepsis screening tool - 1 point each for:

- Respiratory rate ≥ 22/min

- Altered mentation (GCS < 15)

- Systolic blood pressure ≤ 100 mmHg

CURB-65

Pneumonia severity/disposition tool - 1 point each for:

- Confusion (new onset)

- Urea > 7 mmol/L (BUN > 19 mg/dL)

- Respiratory rate ≥ 30

- Blood pressure (systolic < 90 or diastolic ≤ 60)

- Age ≥ 65

Estimates likelihood of Group A Streptococcal pharyngitis - 1 point each for:

- Tonsillar exudate

- Tender anterior cervical lymphadenopathy

- Fever by history

- Absence of cough

.abcd2_score

ABCD2 Score

Estimates 2-day stroke risk after TIA.

- Age ≥ 60: 1 point

- Blood pressure ≥ 140/90 at evaluation: 1 point

- Clinical features: unilateral weakness (2 points), speech disturbance without weakness (1 point), other (0 points)

- Duration: ≥ 60 min (2 points), 10-59 min (1 point), < 10 min (0 points)

- Diabetes: 1 point

Score 0-3: low risk (2-day stroke risk ~1%). Score 4-5: moderate risk (~4.1%). Score 6-7: high risk (~8.1%) - generally warrants admission/expedited workup.

ABCD2 has known limitations (does not incorporate imaging findings or atrial fibrillation) - many centers now use it alongside, not instead of, expedited TIA imaging pathways.

- Anorexia: 1

- Nausea/vomiting: 1

- Tenderness in RLQ: 2

- Rebound pain: 1

.charting_support_mdm_level

Charting to Support the MDM Level Billed

Number/complexity of problems: name each problem actively managed this visit, not just the chief complaint - a chronic illness with exacerbation, or multiple stable chronic illnesses being actively considered in the differential, both count.

Data reviewed: explicitly document review of old records, discussion with another treating provider (by phone, in person, or via the chart), and independent interpretation of imaging/EKG obtained elsewhere (not just the official read) - these are easy to do but easy to forget to write down.

Risk: state the risk explicitly in the MDM - prescription drug management, the specific procedure's risk, or the reasoning behind a disposition decision (admit vs. discharge, resuscitate vs. comfort care) - risk is often present in the chart implicitly but needs to be named to count.

.brief_resolved_unexplained_event

Brief Resolved Unexplained Event (BRUE)

A brief resolved unexplained event (BRUE) happens suddenly and can be frightening. It is a diagnosis given after your baby's doctor has examined your baby and found no known concerning cause for the event. During a BRUE, a baby may seem to stop breathing, change skin color, tense or go limp, or seem to pass out, then recover on their own within a short time.

Lower-risk features: older than 60 days; born on time; did not need CPR; the event lasted less than 1 minute; this was the only such event.

Common questions: A BRUE does not increase the risk of SIDS, though a safe sleep environment is always important (see healthychildren.org/safesleep). Babies at lower risk do not need to stay in the hospital, do not need blood tests or X-ray imaging, and do not need home cardiac or respiratory monitoring. No special care is needed afterward - continue to care for your baby normally.

If it happens again: call 911 if it feels life-threatening; otherwise call your baby's doctor. It's a good idea for every caregiver to learn infant CPR.

Adapted from AAP patient education material, 2016.

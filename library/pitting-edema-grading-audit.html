Pitting Edema Grades: Four Scales Wearing One Name

An EM:RAP / CorePendium graphic on grading pitting edema. The idea is sound. The numbers contradict themselves in two places, and the bigger issue is that every reference uses a different set.

R. E. Suscitate & A. Systole · November 2026. Original post: @emrapshow, “CorePendium Media Highlight: How do you grade pitting edema?” This page is an independent third-party evaluation, not affiliated with or endorsed by the original creator.

Verdict: the 1+ panel labels itself “2 mm” and “<2 mm” at once, and the 2+/3+ depth ranges leave gaps. Beyond the graphic, no grading scale for pitting edema has been validated, and published tables disagree on both depth and rebound time. Document the depth, rebound time and level of edema rather than a grade alone.

01

There is no single scale

The post presents one version of the 1+ to 4+ scale as if it were the standard. It is one of several. Textbooks, nursing references and trial protocols assign different depths and rebound times to the same grade, and some editions of the most-used physical exam text give no numbers at all.

Same grade, different definitions. The 3+ row alone spans “30 seconds” to “more than a minute.” Common-table values follow EBM Consult; trial-protocol values follow a Cushing’s trial protocol on ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT03621280).

03

Examining and recording it so the next person can compare

1

Press over bone

Dorsum of the foot, behind the medial malleolus, and the shin. Firm thumb pressure for at least 2 seconds, then time the rebound from release.

2

Write the level and both numbers

“Pitting to mid-shin bilaterally, ~4 mm, rebound 20 s” carries more than “2+”, and survives a change of examiner or a different reference card.

3

Trend with a tape measure

Ankle circumference at a marked point took a minute and had the best agreement between examiners in Brodovicz’s study. Use it when diuresis or a DVT question depends on change over days.

4

Notice how fast the pit fills

Henry and Altmann (1978) proposed that rapid pit recovery suggests hypoproteinemic edema, separating it from the slow recovery of venous or cardiac edema. Their exact timing threshold is not in the PubMed record and was not checked here; treat it as a prompt to send an albumin.

5

Check for edema that doesn’t pit

Lymphedema, myxedema and lipedema pit poorly or not at all. A negative pit test does not mean no edema.

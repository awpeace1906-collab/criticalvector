Anesthesia Breathing Circuits

R. E. Suscitate & A. Systole

Two families do almost all the work: the circle system, which absorbs CO₂, and the Mapleson systems, which wash it out with fresh gas. A widely shared poster blends the two into categories that don't exist.

VERIFIEDCirculating poster: taxonomy wrongMapleson 1954 · Kaul & Mittal 2013
WRONG MAP

The poster presents four circuits: circle, semi-closed, non-rebreathing Bain, and a "Mapleson (modified Bain)" with a CO₂ absorber. The first three labels describe how a circuit is run, not different hardware, and the fourth circuit doesn't exist. Its clinical pearls about valves and the APL are sound; its circuit descriptions are not.

OpenNo reservoir, no rebreathing. Open drop, insufflation.
Semi-openReservoir bag, no absorber; fresh gas flow prevents rebreathing. Mapleson A–F, including the Bain.
Semi-closedCircle with absorber; partial rebreathing, excess gas leaves via the APL valve.
ClosedCircle with the APL valve closed; fresh gas flow equals the patient's uptake.

Semi-closed and closed are the same circle system at different fresh gas flows. Turn the flow down and a semi-closed circle becomes a low-flow, then a closed, circle.

01

Claim by claim

WRONG
Bain: non-rebreathing circuit.The Bain is a coaxial Mapleson D. Whether it rebreathes depends entirely on fresh gas flow: at too low a flow, exhaled gas is rebreathed and CO₂ rises. It has no valves that make it non-rebreathing.
WRONG
Mapleson (modified Bain): adds a CO₂ absorber.Mapleson systems by definition have no absorber. They clear CO₂ only by fresh gas flow. A circuit with an absorber and one-way valves is a circle system.
WRONG
Semi-closed circuit as a separate circuit type.Semi-closed describes a circle system run with fresh gas flow above the patient's uptake, with excess vented through the APL valve. The poster's semi-closed diagram is a circle system.
OVERSTATED
Circle system (closed system): requires closed APL valve.Only a truly closed circle has the APL valve shut. In everyday practice the circle runs semi-closed, with the APL open to scavenging.
OVERSTATED
Mapleson flows of 3–6 L/min; Bain 6–10 L/min.Mapleson flow requirements depend on the system and on whether ventilation is spontaneous or controlled, and scale with body size. A single flow range per circuit is misleading (see section 02).
CORRECT
Check one-way valves; APL valve controls pressure and directs gas to scavenging.Correct. An incompetent circle valve causes rebreathing and rising inspired CO₂.
02

Mapleson A–F

Fresh gas flow (FGF) needed to prevent rebreathing, as multiples of minute ventilation (MV). Approximate; adult values.

SystemCommon nameSpontaneousControlledUse
AMagill, Lack (coaxial)≈ 1 × alveolar MV (~70 mL/kg/min)2–3 × MV, inefficientBest for spontaneous breathing
B, CC = "Waters" bagging circuit1.5–2 × MV1.5–2 × MVResuscitation and transport bagging (C)
DBain (coaxial D)2–3 × MV (150–200 mL/kg/min)1–2 × MV (70–100 mL/kg/min)Best for controlled ventilation
EAyre's T-piece2.5–3 × MVIntermittent occlusionHistorical pediatric use
FJackson-Rees2.5–3 × MV~2 × MVPediatrics, transport

Efficiency, the classic recall. Spontaneous: A > D, F, E > C, B. Controlled: D, F, E > B, C > A. Mapleson A wins when the patient breathes; D wins when you breathe for them.

Bain-specific hazard. If the inner fresh-gas tube disconnects or kinks, the whole outer limb becomes dead space and the patient rebreathes. The Pethick test (occlude, fill the bag, then release with high oxygen flush) checks inner-tube integrity before use.

03

The circle

Two one-way valves, an inspiratory and expiratory limb, a CO₂ absorber, a reservoir bag, and an APL valve. Because CO₂ is absorbed chemically, fresh gas flow can be cut far below minute ventilation. That conserves volatile agent and preserves heat and humidity.

ModeTypical FGFPractical point
High flow> 2 L/minFast changes in delivered concentration; wasteful
Low flow≈ 0.5–1 L/minEconomical; inspired concentration lags the dial, so monitor end-tidal agent and FiO₂
Closed= uptake (≈ 3–4 mL/kg/min O₂)APL closed; needs a leak-free circuit and continuous gas analysis

Exhausted absorbent shows as a rising inspired CO₂ on the capnogram. Desiccated absorbent can degrade volatiles; keep it moist and change it on schedule.

04

Pearls that hold

  • Check both circle valves at the machine check; a stuck-open valve causes rebreathing.
  • The APL valve sets circuit pressure during spontaneous and bag ventilation and sends excess gas to scavenging.
  • Inspired CO₂ above zero means rebreathing: exhausted absorbent, an incompetent valve, or, in a Mapleson system, too little fresh gas.
  • Choose by setting: circle for most anesthetics; Mapleson C or F for bagging and transport, where there's no absorber to carry.

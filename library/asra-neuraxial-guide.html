01

What's New in the 5th Edition

"Low dose" and "high dose" replace the old "prophylactic" and "therapeutic" language.

New: a drug level can clear a block on its own. DOAC under 30 ng/mL, or anti-Xa activity 0.1 IU/mL or less, is acceptable regardless of time elapsed.

New antiplatelet hold time: ticagrelor (a P2Y12 inhibitor, not a DOAC) is now 5 days, down from the prior 5 to 7 day range.

Reversal agents (andexanet alfa, idarucizumab, PCC) are explicitly not for enabling a block in routine patients. Same goal either way: full clotting recovery before the needle.

02

First Principles

Spinal hematoma is rare, but catastrophic in a fixed, closed space, the epidural space has nowhere for a bleed to expand except onto the cord.

ASRA stays deliberately "antihemorrhagic": the aim is full hemostasis, not a convenient compromise.

Deep plexus and deep peripheral blocks follow the same rules as neuraxial blocks. This is not just a spine issue.

Aspirin or NSAIDs alone: no restriction, proceed.

Most DOAC-specific advice remains grade 2C, "we suggest." Judgment still matters; these are not hard stops.

04

DOACs: Levels & Restart

Can't wait? A residual level can clear the block on its own: under 30 ng/mL, or anti-Xa 0.1 IU/mL or less.

PT and aPTT do not rule out a DOAC effect. These are the wrong tests for this question; don't let a normal PT/aPTT create false reassurance.

Restart low dose (prophylaxis): about 6 hours after the procedure.

Restart high dose: 24 hours or more, 48 to 72 hours after high-bleed-risk surgery.

High-dose DOAC: remove the needle or catheter 24 hours before the next dose, not after.

05

Unfractionated Heparin

IV heparin: stop 4 to 6 hours, with normal coags confirmed, before the block.

Give IV heparin no sooner than 1 hour after needle placement.

Low-dose SC (5,000 U): wait 4 to 6 hours, or confirm normal coags.

High-dose SC: 12 hours (up to 20,000 U/day) or 24 hours (over 20,000 U/day), plus a normal aPTT.

On UFH for more than 4 days? Check platelets first. Heparin-induced thrombocytopenia risk rises with duration of exposure.

06

LMW Heparin

Low (prophylactic) dose: wait 12 hours before the block.

High (therapeutic) dose: wait 24 hours before the block.

Restart prophylaxis: 4 hours or more after catheter removal.

Restart therapeutic: 24 hours, or 48 to 72 hours after high-bleed surgery.

On LMWH for more than 4 days, check platelets. CrCl under 30: double the wait times above.

08

Warfarin, Fondaparinux, Lytics

Warfarin: stop 5 or more days, with a normalized INR. Remove the catheter once INR is under 1.5.

Fondaparinux, low dose (2.5 mg): 36 to 42 hours (58 hours if CrCl 30 to 50, avoid entirely if under 30).

Fondaparinux, high dose (5 to 10 mg): 70 hours in younger patients, 105 hours in the elderly.

Thrombolytics: avoid the needle for 48 hours or more, and confirm normal clotting including fibrinogen before proceeding.

Herbal supplements (garlic, ginkgo, ginseng) do not need to be stopped for a block.

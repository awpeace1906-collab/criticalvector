Mild Hypercapnia After Cardiac Arrest Didn’t Hurt the Right Ventricle

A REBEL EM carousel on the TAME right-ventricle substudy. The headline numbers are right. Two slides need a footnote: one percentage set uses a smaller denominator, and the hypercapnia mortality hazard ratio is not significant.

R. E. Suscitate & A. Systole · November 2026. Original post: @rebelemergencymedicine, “Hypercapnia and…” (carousel, slides 2–3), linking the REBEL EM blog post by Dr. Jad Dandashi (rebelem.com/hypercapnia-on-rv). This page is an independent third-party evaluation, not affiliated with or endorsed by the original creator.

Verdict: RV dysfunction 25% vs 64%, RV failure 8 vs 22, and the RV-failure mortality hazard ratio of 2.89 all match Melberg et al. (Chest 2026). The hypercapnia HR of 0.49 has a confidence interval of 0.21–1.14 and should not be read as a survival benefit. The parent TAME trial (n = 1,700) found no difference in neurologic outcome or death.

Single-center, preplanned substudy of TAME at Oslo University Hospital Ullevål (Melberg et al., Chest 2026). 138 patients were randomized at the site, 111 had echocardiography (52 hypercapnia, 59 normocapnia), and 84 also had time-matched right heart catheterization. PaCO2 targets were 50–55 mm Hg versus 35–45 mm Hg for 24 hours. Everyone was cooled to 33 °C.

The hypercapnia arm had higher stroke volume and cardiac index, lower SVRI, lower pulmonary effective arterial elastance, better RV stroke work and TAPSE/SPAP coupling, and received less fluid. PVRI and CVP did not differ. The fear that permissive hypercapnia loads the post-arrest RV was not borne out here.

What changes at the bedside

Normocapnia stays the post-arrest target. TAME settled the brain question. What this substudy changes is the reflex to hyperventilate a post-arrest patient with a struggling RV: a PaCO2 drifting into the low 50s during lung-protective ventilation did not hurt the RV in these 111 patients, and may have helped by unloading the systemic circulation.

The RV failure signal is the durable finding. TAPSE was the only single parameter that held after adjustment, so an early bedside echo with TAPSE, S′ and FAC, plus a CVP and an estimate of cardiac index, identifies the patients at highest risk. The authors call the result hypothesis generating, and it is.

Macrolides: Azithromycin, Clarithromycin, Erythromycin

R. E. Suscitate & A. Systole

One class, one ribosomal target, three very different drugs at the bedside. What separates them is half-life, CYP3A4 inhibition, and which indications still hold up against current guidelines.

01

Corrections to the circulating chart

A widely shared comparison chart gets the pharmacokinetics mostly right and the indications out of date.

CHARTAzithromycin rated "moderate" against S. pneumoniae, clarithromycin and erythromycin "good."
CURRENTThe main resistance mechanisms (erm methylation, mef efflux) cross the class. ATS/IDSA 2019 allows macrolide monotherapy for outpatient CAP only where pneumococcal macrolide resistance is under 25%, which rules it out in most of the US.
CHARTClarithromycin elimination listed as hepatic (CYP3A4) only.
CURRENTA large share of clarithromycin and its active 14-hydroxy metabolite is cleared by the kidney. That is why the dose is halved below CrCl 30.
CHARTAzithromycin listed for chlamydial urethritis and cervicitis without qualification.
CURRENTCDC 2021: doxycycline 100 mg twice daily for 7 days is preferred. Azithromycin 1 g once is the alternative, and the recommended choice in pregnancy.
CHARTClarithromycin for H. pylori eradication in combination therapy.
CURRENTACG 2024 advises against empiric clarithromycin triple therapy. Bismuth quadruple is first line unless clarithromycin susceptibility has been confirmed.
CHARTNo boxed or regulatory safety warnings mentioned.
CURRENTFDA 2013: azithromycin and risk of fatal arrhythmia. FDA 2018: higher long-term mortality after clarithromycin in patients with heart disease. Both belong on any comparison of these drugs.

What the chart has right: the half-lives, the CYP3A4 ranking, MAC coverage, and erythromycin's prokinetic role.

02

Where each one earns its place

Azithromycin

The workhorse. Atypical coverage for inpatient CAP alongside a β-lactam, pertussis, enteric infections, and long-term COPD exacerbation prevention. Few drug interactions, once-daily dosing, and a tissue half-life that makes short courses work.

Clarithromycin

A backbone of MAC treatment, and H. pylori therapy when susceptibility is known. Otherwise the interaction burden usually makes azithromycin the better choice.

Erythromycin

Now mostly a motilin agonist. A single IV dose 30–120 minutes before endoscopy in upper GI bleeding clears the stomach and improves the view. Also used for gastroparesis, with tachyphylaxis over weeks.

03

Interactions and QT

Clarithromycin and erythromycin raise CYP3A4 substrate levels. Simvastatin and lovastatin are contraindicated (rhabdomyolysis). Colchicine can reach fatal levels, especially with renal or hepatic impairment. Expect higher apixaban and rivaroxaban exposure.

Calcium channel blockers. In older adults, clarithromycin with a CCB was associated with more hypotension and acute kidney injury than azithromycin with the same CCB.

QT risk adds up. Check the baseline QTc, potassium, and magnesium before stacking a macrolide with ondansetron, haloperidol, a fluoroquinolone, or methadone. IV erythromycin carries the highest arrhythmia risk of the three.

Infants. Erythromycin in the first weeks of life is linked to hypertrophic pyloric stenosis. Azithromycin is the preferred agent for pertussis under 1 month of age.

04

Class features

All three bind the 50S ribosomal subunit and block protein synthesis. They are bacteriostatic at usual doses and concentrate inside cells, which is why they cover intracellular organisms like Legionella, Chlamydia, and Mycoplasma. GI upset is the most common adverse effect. It is a direct motilin effect, and strongest with erythromycin.

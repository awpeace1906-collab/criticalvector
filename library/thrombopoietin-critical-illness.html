Thrombopoietin for Thrombocytopenia in Critical Illness

R. E. Suscitate & A. Systole

A narrative review found eleven studies of thrombopoietin-directed therapy in critically ill patients, mostly in sepsis and mostly with a drug Americans can't get. The signal is faster platelet recovery; the evidence is preliminary.

01

The signal

The authors conclude that limited data suggest thrombopoietin may benefit critically ill patients with thrombocytopenia, and that more studies are needed. In their summary of the sepsis literature:

  • Faster platelet recovery, often beginning by day 3 and peaking around day 7.
  • Fewer platelet transfusions and better coagulation profiles in some studies.
  • A meta-analysis of 10 randomized trials (681 patients) found higher platelet counts by day 7 and a possible reduction in transfusion.
  • A possible survival benefit in one randomized trial and one retrospective cohort.

The figures above come from the authors' summary of the full text, not the abstract.

Whether romiplostim would reproduce the rhTPO results is unknown.

03

Limits

  • Slow. Platelet effects take days, so these agents cannot replace hemorrhage control or platelet transfusion.
  • Thrombotic risk remains a concern.
  • No trial in trauma-associated thrombocytopenia, despite the review's trauma framing.
  • Heterogeneous, mostly observational evidence, largely with a non-US drug.

Bottom line: an interesting hypothesis for sepsis-associated thrombocytopenia, not a therapy. Not ready for routine use.

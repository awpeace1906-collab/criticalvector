Why Your Trauma Patient Is Dying (And What You Can Do About It)

This ain't your standard code blue. Traumatic cardiac arrest has different causes and a different algorithm — treating it like a medical code is a common, fixable mistake.

Original post: definshock. This page is an independent third-party verification, not affiliated with or endorsed by the account.

Verdict: exactly right. Standard ACLS logic (epinephrine, rhythm-focused algorithm, prolonged compressions) is a poor fit for traumatic cardiac arrest, where the cause is almost always a mechanical, reversible problem — and the evidence supports treating the reversible cause before, or instead of, running a standard code.
01

Why This Isn't a Standard Code

Medical cardiac arrest is usually a primary electrical or ischemic cardiac event. Traumatic cardiac arrest is usually the heart failing secondary to a mechanical problem elsewhere — and compressions on an empty or obstructed heart don't fix the actual cause.

Chest compressions generate cardiac output by mechanically squeezing a heart that has adequate preload to move. In traumatic arrest, the dominant causes — severe hypovolemia from hemorrhage, tension pneumothorax obstructing venous return, and cardiac tamponade compressing the heart directly — all mean the heart has nothing to pump, or can't fill, regardless of how well compressions are performed. Compressions on a heart with no preload are far less likely to generate meaningful cardiac output than in a primary cardiac arrest, which is why guidelines increasingly emphasize identifying and treating the reversible cause as the actual resuscitation, not a secondary consideration alongside compressions.

The HOTT Framework

CauseInterventionPriority
HypovolemiaBlood products, hemorrhage control (external pressure, tourniquets, pelvic binder), resuscitative thoracotomy/REBOA in the right settingMost common reversible cause in blunt and penetrating trauma arrest
OxygenationAirway control, ventilation — hypoxia as arrest cause or as a compounding factorAddress concurrently, not sequentially
Tension pneumothoraxBilateral finger/needle thoracostomy — don't wait for confirmatory imaging in arrestRapidly reversible, easily missed if not actively sought
TamponadePericardiocentesis or resuscitative thoracotomy with pericardiotomy depending on setting and resourcesClassically underrecognized; think of it specifically in penetrating chest trauma

The HOTT causes run concurrently with, not after, standard airway/breathing/circulation assessment — and in traumatic arrest, treating these mechanical causes takes priority over the reflexive administration of epinephrine, which does not address the actual physiologic problem and has not shown the same evidence base in traumatic arrest that it has in medical arrest.

03

Bottom Line

The source post's core message is accurate and important: traumatic cardiac arrest is a different disease from medical cardiac arrest and needs a different mental model. Bilateral chest decompression, aggressive hemorrhage control and blood products, and a high index of suspicion for tamponade should take priority over running a standard ACLS algorithm — because in trauma, the arrest is almost always a symptom of a specific, findable, treatable mechanical problem, not a primary cardiac event.

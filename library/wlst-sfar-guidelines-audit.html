Withholding and Withdrawing Life Support: A French Flowchart, Read in Context

Critical Care Reviews shared the decision flowchart from the 2026 SFAR guidelines on limiting life-sustaining treatment. The figure is faithful. The post never says it describes French law.

R. E. Suscitate & A. Systole · November 2026. Original post: @criticalcarereviews, CCR Journal Watch. This page is an independent third-party evaluation, not affiliated with or endorsed by the original creator.

Verdict: correctly reproduced from Giabicani et al. (SFAR/SOFMER, 2026). The collegial procedure, external consultant, appeal window and continuous deep sedation (“CDS”) are requirements of the French Claeys–Leonetti framework. Of the nine recommendations, only one is GRADE 1: a structured support strategy for families.

01

Whose guideline this is

The flowchart comes from the French Society of Anaesthesia and Intensive Care Medicine (SFAR) with the French Society of Physical Medicine and Rehabilitation (SOFMER), published in Anaesthesia Critical Care & Pain Medicine (Giabicani et al., 2026). The French text adds the French palliative care society (SFAP). It was written to operate inside the Claeys–Leonetti law of 2016, and several boxes in the figure are legal requirements in France rather than clinical recommendations that travel.

Simplified from the guideline text and French law; the post’s figure shows the full branching.
Box in the figureWhat it means in FranceUS equivalent
Collegial decision-makingMandatory before withholding or withdrawing for a patient without capacityTeam consensus is good practice; no statutory procedure
External physician (“consultant”)At least one physician with no hierarchical link to the treating team must give a reasoned opinionNot required; ethics consultation is available but optional in most states
Trusted person (personne de confiance)Consulted and informed; does not decide. The physician decides.The surrogate decides, using substituted judgment or best interest
Time frame for appealSince a 2017 constitutional ruling, relatives must be told in time to seek emergency judicial review (référé-liberté); the judge rules within 48 hNo routine waiting period; disputes go through ethics, then courts
CDSContinuous deep sedation maintained until death (SPCMD), a statutory right under defined conditions; titrated to RASS −4 to −5Palliative sedation is practiced, but with no specific statutory right

No recommendation on extubation versus terminal weaning, or on mediation teams: the panel found too little evidence. The one strong recommendation is about families, not procedure.

04

What transfers

Portable to any ICU

Structured, proactive support for families is the best-evidenced piece here, rated GRADE 1, and it needs no legal scaffolding. So does involving bedside nurses in the decision meeting, and writing down a sedation protocol for withdrawal before you need one.

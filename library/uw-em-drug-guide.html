Emergency Medicine Drug Guide: Pediatric & Adult

Built from a UW Health Kids pediatric emergency card, expanded with adult dosing throughout and organized for actual EM use: a function-based quick-recall table up front (vasopressors, antiarrhythmics, paralytics, reversal agents, and more, all in one scan), then full clinical-scenario context after. Verified against current ACLS/PALS, status epilepticus, and toxicology literature — including a real 2026 dosing change and an adult/peds divergence worth knowing. Verified against AHA, ANZCOR, and primary emergency medicine literature. July 2026.

Two things changed or diverged recently and are flagged where they appear: the 2026 AHA update doubled the adult atropine starting dose for symptomatic bradycardia from 0.5 mg to 1 mg, and vasopressin has been removed from the adult ACLS algorithm entirely — while pediatric arrest protocols (including the source card) still use it. Don't cross-apply doses between the two populations.

Seizures / Status Epilepticus

Pediatric

Lorazepam0.05–0.1 mg/kg IV/IO
Midazolam0.3 mg/kg IN (max 10 mg)
Diazepam0.05–0.1 mg/kg IV/IO; 0.3–0.5 mg/kg PR
Phenobarbital20 mg/kg IV/IO
Fosphenytoin20 mg PE/kg IV/IO/IM
Levetiracetam20 mg/kg IV/IO

Adult

Lorazepam4 mg IV push (up to 8 mg in alcohol use/high BMI); repeat q2–3 min
Midazolam10 mg IM if no IV access (RAMPART protocol)
Diazepam10–20 mg IV (0.15 mg/kg, up to 10 mg per some guidelines)
Fosphenytoin20 mg PE/kg IV (max ~1500 mg PE)
Levetiracetam60 mg/kg IV (max 4500 mg) — increasingly favored second-line agent

The most common real-world failure isn't drug choice — it's underdosing. A 2025 ED cohort study found guideline-adherent initial benzodiazepine dosing in only 17% of patients, with inadequate dosing rates of 77% for lorazepam, 90% for midazolam, and 97% for diazepam — and inadequate total dosing was independently associated with higher in-hospital mortality. Push the full weight-based or fixed adult dose; don't split it "to be safe."

Anaphylaxis

Pediatric

Epinephrine0.01 mg/kg IM (1 mg/mL) (max 0.5 mg/dose)
Diphenhydramine1 mg/kg IV/IO/IM/PO (max 50 mg)
Methylprednisolone2 mg/kg IV/IO/IM initial (max 125 mg)

Adult

Epinephrine0.3–0.5 mg IM (1 mg/mL), anterolateral thigh — repeat q5–15 min as needed

Methylprednisolone125 mg IV (or prednisone 40–60 mg PO if tolerating oral)
Famotidine20 mg IV (ranitidine largely withdrawn from the US market since 2020 — famotidine is the current H2 blocker of choice)

Epinephrine is the only intervention proven to reduce mortality in anaphylaxis — antihistamines and steroids treat symptoms and prevent biphasic reactions but are not substitutes for epi, in either population.

Croup is overwhelmingly a pediatric condition (narrow subglottic airway). Adult upper airway obstruction from other causes (angioedema, epiglottitis, anaphylaxis) is managed per the specific etiology — nebulized racemic epinephrine is used off-label in adult post-extubation stridor at similar dosing, with dexamethasone 10 mg IV as a common adjunct.

Cardiac Arrest & Electrical Therapy

Two recent changes worth knowing precisely: the 2026 AHA update doubled the adult atropine starting dose for symptomatic bradycardia from 0.5 mg to 1 mg IV, repeated q3–5 min to a max of 3 mg. Separately, vasopressin has been removed from the adult ACLS cardiac arrest algorithm — ANZCOR explicitly suggests against substituting vasopressin for epinephrine or adding it alongside epinephrine, since combined trial evidence (PARAMEDIC2, PACA) showed no neurological outcome benefit. Pediatric arrest protocols, including the source card here, still specify vasopressin 0.5 units/kg — the two populations have genuinely diverged on this drug.

Pediatric

Epinephrine0.01 mg/kg IV/IO (0.1 mg/mL); 0.1 mg/kg ETT (1 mg/mL)
Amiodarone5 mg/kg IV/IO push, refractory VT/VF (max 300 mg)
Lidocaine1 mg/kg IV/IO bolus; 2–3 mg/kg ETT
Vasopressin0.5 units/kg IV/IO (max 40 units)
Defibrillation2 J/kg, then 4 J/kg (do not exceed adult max)
Synchronized cardioversion0.5–1 J/kg, then 2 J/kg

Adult

Epinephrine1 mg IV/IO q3–5 min
Amiodarone300 mg IV/IO first dose, 150 mg second dose, refractory VF/pVT
Lidocaine1–1.5 mg/kg IV/IO, alternative to amiodarone
Atropine1 mg IV q3–5 min, max 3 mg (2026 update — was 0.5 mg)
VasopressinRemoved from current adult ACLS algorithm
DefibrillationBiphasic, manufacturer-recommended (commonly 120–200 J)
Synchronized cardioversionPer rhythm — narrow regular 50–100J, narrow irregular/wide 120–200J

Calcium chloride (10–30 mg/kg peds; 1 g adult, central line preferred) and sodium bicarbonate (1–2 mEq/kg peds; 1 mEq/kg adult) are not routine cardiac arrest drugs in either population per current guidance — reserved specifically for suspected hyperkalemia, calcium channel blocker toxicity, or severe pre-existing acidosis.

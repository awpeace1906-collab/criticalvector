Troponin Is a Symptom, Not a Diagnosis

01

Injury vs. Infarction

Myocardial injury: at least one cardiac troponin (cTn) value above the 99th-percentile upper reference limit (URL). Nothing else is required. Acute if rising/falling on serials; chronic if stable (≤20% variation).

Myocardial infarction requires injury plus evidence of ischemia — at least one of:

  • Ischemic symptoms
  • New ischemic ECG changes
  • New pathological Q waves
  • Imaging evidence of new loss of viable myocardium or new regional wall motion abnormality in an ischemic pattern
  • Angiographic or autopsy-identified intracoronary thrombus

This two-part structure — injury as necessary but not sufficient — is the load-bearing concept of the Fourth UDMI and is unchanged in the Fifth.

02

Why the Number Lies to You Alone

An elevated troponin says the myocyte membrane failed somewhere. It doesn't say why. Table 1 of the Fourth UDMI lists the mechanism categories explicitly:

CategoryExamples
Cardiac, non-ACSHeart failure, myocarditis, tachy-/brady-arrhythmias, stress cardiomyopathy
Critical illnessSepsis, distributive/cardiogenic shock, multi-organ failure
PulmonaryPE, severe pneumonia, ARDS/respiratory failure
RenalCKD (reduced clearance), AKI
NeurologicIschemic/hemorrhagic stroke, SAH, TBI (catecholamine-mediated)
ProceduralPost-PCI, post-ablation, post-cardioversion, cardiac surgery
Supply–demand mismatchSevere anemia, hypotension, sustained tachyarrhythmia, hypertensive emergency
OtherStrenuous exertion, infiltrative disease (amyloid, sarcoid), cardiotoxic chemo, burns

Not decorative — this is the differential you're running at the bedside every time a resus or ICU troponin comes back hot before you've called a STEMI/NSTEMI activation.

03

What Changed in 2026, Corrected

Fifth Universal Definition of Myocardial Infarction — Mills NL, Newby LK, Zaman S, et al.; ESC/ACC/AHA/WHF Universal Definition Document Advisory Group. Published August 28, 2026, European Heart Journal, jointly issued by ESC/ACC/AHA/WHF, endorsed by EACTS/STS, affirmed by SCAI.

It retires the familiar Type 1 / 2 / 3 / 4a–c / 5 taxonomy and replaces it with three clinical categories:

New categoryReplacesDefinition
Primary MIType 1Spontaneous, driven by acute coronary pathology — most commonly atherothrombotic plaque disruption, but the document explicitly allows non-atherothrombotic coronary pathologies identified by imaging/functional testing
Secondary MIType 2Driven by an acute non-coronary condition causing oxygen supply–demand mismatch
Procedure-related MIType 4/5 familyTied to PCI or cardiac surgery, with objective criteria for when peri-procedural troponin rise crosses into infarction territory

Two changes beyond the renaming have real bedside teeth:

  • Sex-specific troponin thresholds are built into the definition itself, not left as an assay-dependent footnote
  • Imaging is explicitly promoted to establish MI etiology once injury + ischemia criteria are met — pushing practice further from ECG-and-troponin-only adjudication, especially in ambiguous or MINOCA-type presentations

Dedicated ICD-11 codes are also proposed to standardize downstream coding and research use of the new taxonomy.

04

A Working Framework (Fifth UDMI–Aligned)

1. Is there myocardial injury? cTn above the sex-specific 99th-percentile URL.

2. Acute or chronic? Look at the delta on serials, not the single number.

3. Is there evidence of ischemia? Symptoms, ECG, imaging, angiographic/pathologic findings.

4. If MI is confirmed, which clinical category? Primary, secondary, or procedure-related.

5. Do you need more information to establish etiology? Echo, CCTA, CMR, or invasive angiography.

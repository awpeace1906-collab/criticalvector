Heart Rate Variability in the ICU: Real Signal, Thin Outcome Data

An Anesthesiology journal post promoting a new review of HRV methods in critical illness. The citation and the figure check out. The caption’s “prognosis and treatment” runs ahead of the adult evidence.

R. E. Suscitate & A. Systole · November 2026. Original post: @anesthesiology_journal, October 26, 2026, promoting Bharadwaj et al. This page is an independent third-party evaluation, not affiliated with or endorsed by the original creator.

Verdict: the review exists as cited (Anesthesiology, November 2026). The baroreflex figure is correct but incomplete. The only randomized trial showing an outcome benefit from HRV-type monitoring was in premature neonates; adult critical care data remain prognostic and observational.

01

The figure

The diagram shows glossopharyngeal afferents from the carotid sinus to the brainstem, vagal efferents to the heart, and sympathetic fibers to the heart and a vessel. That is the baroreflex arc, and the parts shown are drawn correctly. Two clarifications: the carotid body is a chemoreceptor, not a baroreceptor, although both travel in CN IX; and the aortic arch baroreceptors, carried by the vagus, are left out.

The post’s figure shows the carotid limb only. The aortic arch afferent runs in the vagus. HRV in the high-frequency band mostly reflects the fast vagal efferent.

Adult sepsis signal
17 bone marrow transplant patients: HRV fell before clinical sepsis in 12 of 14 who became infected. Feasibility only.

The gap between those two boxes is the state of the field. Bedside HRV in adults is a research tool that may become a monitoring channel; it is not yet something to act on in isolation.

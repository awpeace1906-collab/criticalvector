Antidromic Mahaim Tachycardia: Confirmed, Plus the Named Criteria Set

01

The Physiology, Confirmed

Mahaim (atriofascicular) pathways are a genuinely distinct entity from typical WPW accessory pathways, and the post's circuit description is textbook-accurate.

Atriofascicular Mahaim fibers connect the right atrium to the distal right bundle branch/RV myocardium, are classically decrementally conducting (like the AV node) and antegrade-only (unlike typical WPW pathways, which usually conduct in both directions). During antidromic AVRT, the Mahaim pathway is necessarily the antegrade limb (RA→pathway→RV) with the AV node/His-Purkinje system completing the circuit retrogradely — exactly as the post describes. Because ventricular activation starts in the right ventricle and propagates toward the left, the resulting QRS mimics a left bundle branch block pattern.

02

The ECG Morphology, Confirmed

Negative V1–V4, late R-wave transition, LBBB-type wide QRS — all textbook-accurate for this mechanism.

Right-to-left ventricular activation via an atriofascicular pathway classically produces: an LBBB-type wide QRS, an rS (negative-dominant) pattern in the early precordial leads as the initial activation vector moves away from V1–V4, and a late R-wave transition typically after V4 (sometimes V5–V6) as activation reaches the posterior/left ventricle late. This matches published descriptions closely, including the specific "late transition after lead V4" language used in differential-diagnosis literature on LBBB-pattern wide-complex tachycardias.

The Named Criteria the Post's Pearls Are Built On

Worth having on hand if you want a citable, validated checklist rather than gestalt pattern recognition.

The Bardy criteria (gold standard for antidromic Mahaim tachycardia)

Six ECG features, together showing high efficacy for distinguishing antidromic Mahaim tachycardia from other wide-complex tachycardias, including VT: (1) QRS axis between 0° and −75° (left axis), (2) QRS duration ≤0.15 sec, (3) R wave in limb lead I, (4) rS pattern in precordial lead V1, (5) precordial transition to a predominantly positive QRS after V4, (6) tachycardia cycle length 220–450 ms. A follow-up validation study found these criteria have 87.5% sensitivity and 82.5% negative predictive value specifically within atriofascicular pathways — the main source of false positives being aberrantly conducted tachycardia with ventriculoatrial conduction over a separate accessory AV pathway.

This maps directly onto the post's own summary: the "negative QRS polarity in inferior leads" clue corresponds to the Bardy criteria's left-axis-deviation requirement (0° to −75° produces negative deflection in the inferior leads II, III, aVF), and the "late R-wave transition in V5–V6" matches criterion 5 almost exactly.

04

The Differential, Confirmed

VT always belongs in the differential for any wide-complex tachycardia, and the post says so correctly.

The post's caution — that a regular LBBB-pattern WCT should raise suspicion for antidromic Mahaim-mediated AVRT but VT must always remain on the differential — is the correct clinical stance. Even the validated Bardy criteria, at 87.5% sensitivity, aren't perfect; the published false-positive/false-negative literature specifically flags aberrantly conducted SVT with an accessory-pathway VA connection as the main confounder, underscoring why pattern criteria alone shouldn't override clinical judgment or, where needed, formal EP study.

05

Bottom Line

This case-based teaching post holds up completely against the EP literature — the circuit description, the mechanism behind the ECG pattern, and the differential diagnosis caveat are all accurate. The one thing worth bookmarking alongside it: the Bardy criteria, the actual named, validated 6-point ECG checklist (axis, QRS duration, lead I, V1 morphology, precordial transition point, cycle length) that formalizes the pattern recognition this post teaches by example.

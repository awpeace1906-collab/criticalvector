Magnesium & Lactate Clearance in Sepsis

World J Crit Care Med. 2026;15(2):115389. Reviewed against the primary trial and prior comparable data.

1 · The question 2 · Trial design 3 · Primary outcome 4 · Secondary findings 5 · Mortality: the honest gap 6 · How this compares 7 · Bottom line & sources

1.The Question

Elevated lactate in sepsis tracks with worse outcomes, and how fast it clears tracks with recovery. Magnesium is a cofactor in the enzymes that run cellular energy metabolism, so the logic is direct: if magnesium is low or borderline, topping it up might help the body clear lactate faster. This trial is the first to specifically test time to 20% lactate clearance as the primary endpoint after magnesium supplementation in sepsis.

2.Trial Design

DesignSingle-center, triple-blind RCT
Population138 adults meeting Sepsis-3 criteria, serum lactate above 2 mmol/L
Intervention2 g magnesium sulfate in 50 mL normal saline over 2 hours, daily for 3 days (Group M)
ControlIdentical-volume saline placebo (Group P)
Primary outcomeTime to 20% lactate clearance from baseline
Secondary outcomes28-day mortality, daily lactate clearance percentage, vasopressor dose, ICU and hospital length of stay

Time to 20% lactate clearance: 5.73 ± 2.19 hours with magnesium versus 8.54 ± 2.72 hours with placebo. Mean difference -2.81 hours (95% CI -3.65 to -1.99), P<.001.

Nearly 3 hours faster to hit the same clearance threshold is a real physiologic signal, not a marginal one.

Vasopressor dose was significantly lower in the magnesium group on days 2 and 3, consistent with magnesium's known effect on vascular tone and microcirculatory perfusion.

5.Mortality: the Honest Gap

28-day mortality was numerically lower in the magnesium group, but the difference did not reach statistical significance. The authors attribute this to the single-center design and modest sample size (138 patients total), not to an absence of effect. This is the same pattern seen in earlier, smaller magnesium trials in sepsis.

A faster physiologic marker moving faster is not the same claim as fewer people dying. Both can be true, but only one of them was actually shown here.

6.How This Compares

Noormandi et al (Eur J Clin Pharmacol, 2020) ran a similar triple-arm comparison in 58 patients with severe sepsis and found the same direction of effect, though the two trials measured "time to clearance" differently, so the raw hour counts are not directly comparable.

Two independent trials, different countries, similar magnitude of effect on the percentage-clearance metric. That consistency is more reassuring than either trial alone.

7.Bottom Line & Sources

Clinical takeaway: in septic patients with an elevated lactate, empiric magnesium supplementation (2 g IV over 2 hours, daily for 3 days) speeds lactate clearance, cuts vasopressor requirements, and shortens ICU and hospital stay in this trial. It has not yet been shown, in an adequately powered trial, to reduce mortality. Treat it as a physiologically plausible adjunct with two consistent positive trials behind it, not as a mortality-proven intervention.

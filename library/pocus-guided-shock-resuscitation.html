POCUS-Guided Shock Resuscitation

R. E. Suscitate & A. Systole

A 2024 meta-analysis of 18 randomized trials found a small mortality signal for ultrasound-guided resuscitation. The signal is real but narrow, rests largely on small trials, and arrives without a clear explanation of what the ultrasound changed.

VERIFIEDBasmaji et al., Crit Care Med 2024SR/MA · 18 RCTs · GRADE

The mortality interval clears 1.0 by a hundredth. The renal replacement therapy interval does not clear it at all.

03

What did not change

  • ICU and hospital admissions, and ICU and hospital length of stay (little to no difference).
  • Need for mechanical ventilation (little to no difference).
  • Number of CT scans (may be no change); diagnostic echocardiograms probably decreased.
  • Acute kidney injury and duration of ventilation or RRT (very low certainty; effect uncertain).

The puzzle. Mortality moved, but the fluids and inotropes delivered barely did. Either the benefit runs through decisions the trials did not measure, such as earlier source control or a corrected diagnosis, or the mortality signal is less robust than its interval suggests. The review cannot say which.

04

The ED question

The trial closest to "undifferentiated shock in the emergency department" is SHoC-ED: 273 hypotensive ED patients across six centers in North America and South Africa, randomized to an early POCUS protocol or standard care. Survival to 30 days or discharge was essentially identical (104/136 vs 102/134; difference 0.35%, 95% CI −10.2% to 11.0%), and a later report found no difference in shock index, lactate, or bicarbonate at four hours.

SHoC-ED was small and cannot exclude benefit. It does mean the strongest ED-specific trial is neutral, and the pooled mortality signal comes from the other trials.

05

At the bedside

  • Use POCUS in shock. The pooled evidence leans toward benefit and away from harm, and it reliably answers questions an exam cannot: tamponade, RV failure, a hyperdynamic empty heart, gross LV dysfunction.
  • Expect it to sharpen decisions, not to replace them. The benefit depends on what you do with the image.
  • Do not cite it as proof that POCUS reduces dialysis; that estimate crosses no effect.
  • Do not quote the mortality effect as settled. A relative risk of 0.88 with an upper bound of 0.99 is a probable small benefit at low-to-moderate certainty.

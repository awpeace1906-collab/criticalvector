TEG Interpretation

A complete teaching guide — the classic TEG 5000 result model and the newer cartridge-based TEG 6s result model, side by side.

What TEG Actually Measures

Thromboelastography (TEG) is a viscoelastic test: it measures the physical strength of a clot as it forms, matures, and breaks down in whole blood, rather than isolating a single factor the way PT/INR or PTT do. A pin (TEG 5000) or an oscillating fluid meniscus (TEG 6s) tracks resistance over time as fibrin strands form and platelets cross-link them, producing the characteristic spindle-shaped tracing.1 Because it evaluates the whole clotting cascade — factors, fibrinogen, platelets, and fibrinolysis — in one test, TEG enables goal-directed, product-specific transfusion rather than empiric ratio-based resuscitation.2

Two generations of hardware exist side by side in most systems today: the older TEG 5000 (a single kaolin-activated cup-and-pin channel per run) and the newer TEG 6s (a disposable four-channel microfluidic cartridge). They report overlapping but not identical parameter sets — this guide covers both, and how to translate between them.

Reading the Raw Tracing

The five classic parameters, mapped onto the actual spindle shape

Every downstream parameter is really just a labeled point on this one curve — worth anchoring visually before the tables below turn it into numbers.

Schematic, not to scale. R = time to initial clot formation · K = time to a fixed early amplitude · α = slope of the widening curve · MA = peak width, i.e. clot strength · LY30 = amplitude lost 30 minutes after MA.

The Classic Model: TEG 5000

Single kaolin-activated channel · cup-and-pin mechanical detection · the five parameters most clinicians learned first

The TEG 5000 reports five core parameters from a single kaolin-activated trace. Each maps to a specific deficiency and a specific blood product, which is what makes TEG useful as a bedside, goal-directed tool rather than a lab curiosity.

ParameterDefinitionNormal RangeReflects / Problem WithTreatment
R TimeTime from sample run to initial fibrin formation (1–2 mm amplitude)5–10 minCoagulation factor activityFFP; PCC if severe/urgent
K TimeTime from end of R to a fixed clot strength (20 mm amplitude)1–3 minFibrinogen (thrombin burst / early cross-linking)Cryoprecipitate
Alpha AngleSlope of the tracing as it widens — speed of fibrin accumulation53–72°Fibrinogen and platelet contribution together (see Pitfalls)Cryoprecipitate ± platelets
MA (Max Amplitude)Widest point of the tracing — ultimate clot strength50–70 mmPlatelet number and function (the dominant driver)Platelets; DDAVP for qualitative dysfunction
LY30% reduction in amplitude 30 minutes after MA0–8%Excess fibrinolysis (breakdown outpacing formation)TXA or aminocaproic acid

Trauma-specific thresholds refine these ranges: an R time beyond roughly twice normal predicts massive transfusion need, and an LY30 above 3% in the injured patient (a lower bar than the general reference range) identifies clinically significant hyperfibrinolysis warranting antifibrinolytic therapy.3

Common Myth The alpha angle is often taught as a pure fibrinogen measurement. It isn't — alpha angle reflects the combined contribution of fibrinogen and platelet function, since both accelerate the rate of clot strengthening. On the TEG 5000 alone, isolated fibrinogen deficiency cannot be cleanly separated from platelet dysfunction using alpha angle. This is precisely the gap the newer TEG 6s functional fibrinogen channel was built to close.

The New Model: TEG 6s

Disposable four-channel cartridge · optical detection · runs multiple activators in parallel from one draw

The TEG 6s replaces the mechanical cup-and-pin with a microfluidic cartridge and optical detection of clot-driven resonance, requiring a single ~400 µL citrated blood transfer instead of manual pipetting across separate cups.4 Because the cartridge runs four channels in parallel from one sample, it reports a fuller hemostatic picture per draw than a single TEG 5000 run — most notably, a dedicated functional fibrinogen channel that the classic model lacks.

ChannelActivatorPrimary Parameter(s)Example Normal Range*What It Isolates
CK (Citrated Kaolin)Kaolin — the direct TEG 5000 analogR, LY30R: 4.6–9.1 min · LY30: 0–3.2%Global factor activity; baseline hyperfibrinolysis screen
CRT / CRTH (Citrated RapidTEG ± heparinase)Kaolin + tissue factorMA, faster RMA: ~52–69 mmRapid overall clot strength when time is critical (e.g. massive transfusion)
CKH (Citrated Kaolin + Heparinase)Kaolin + heparinase (neutralizes heparin)RR: 4.3–8.3 minTrue factor status once heparin effect is removed
CFF / CFFH (Citrated Functional Fibrinogen ± heparinase)Kaolin + tissue factor + a GPIIb/IIIa platelet inhibitorMA (fibrinogen-only)MA: 15–34 mmIsolated fibrinogen contribution to clot strength, with platelets pharmacologically silenced

The CFF channel is the key structural advance: by pharmacologically blocking platelet contribution with a GPIIb/IIIa antagonist, its MA correlates directly with Clauss fibrinogen concentration, giving a true isolated fibrinogen readout that the TEG 5000's alpha angle could only approximate.5 The CK/CKH pairing similarly enables direct heparin detection: comparing CK-R to CKH-R (kaolin alone versus kaolin plus heparin-neutralizing heparinase) isolates a heparin effect that a single TEG 5000 trace cannot resolve on its own.

*Ranges as printed on the manufacturer's 07-604-US (Global Hemostasis-HN) result display. Ranges differ by cartridge and by each lab's validation; use the ranges your lab reports.10

Reading order: Rate → Strength → Strength → Stability R is the rate of clot initiation (factors, heparin). CFF MA is strength from fibrinogen alone. CRT MA is strength from platelets and fibrinogen together. LY30 is stability, the clot's resistance to lysis. Read a result left to right in that order.

Platelet Mapping: Detecting Antiplatelet Drug Effect

A specialized add-on assay, ordered separately from the standard panel

Standard TEG channels fully activate platelets with kaolin or tissue factor, so a maximal thrombin-driven clot forms regardless of whether the patient is taking aspirin or a P2Y12 inhibitor — routine MA cannot see antiplatelet drug effect. Platelet Mapping (TEG-PM) resolves this with two additional heparinized channels that isolate platelet contribution by agonist.

ChannelWhat It IsolatesMA Represents
MAThrombinStandard kaolin-activated channel — full, unblocked platelet activation100% baseline platelet contribution
MAFibrinHeparinized blood + Activator F (reptilase) — platelets chemically silenced0% platelet contribution; fibrin-only clot
MAAAHeparinized blood + Activator F + arachidonic acidPlatelet response via the COX-1/thromboxane pathway — reflects aspirin effect
MAADPHeparinized blood + Activator F + ADPPlatelet response via the P2Y12 receptor — reflects clopidogrel/prasugrel/ticagrelor effect

Percent platelet inhibition is calculated as: 100 − [(MAagonist − MAFibrin) ÷ (MAThrombin − MAFibrin) × 100].6

Clinical Use Perioperative antiplatelet management before neuraxial anesthesia or urgent surgery, identifying clopidogrel non-responders after PCI/stenting, and guiding platelet transfusion when reversing dual antiplatelet therapy for an urgent bleed or intracranial hemorrhage.

Numerical values are not directly interchangeable between platforms: head-to-head comparison during liver transplantation found considerable inter-device variability despite both systems measuring the same underlying physiology, so trend a patient on one platform rather than converting a TEG 5000 result to a TEG 6s threshold or vice versa.4

Crossing Over to ROTEM

Same physiology, different vendor, different assay names — common outside North American trauma centers

Rotational thromboelastometry (ROTEM) assesses the same underlying clot physiology as TEG using a rotating-pin rather than cup-and-pin or optical mechanism, with its own reagent-named assays and parameter labels.1

FunctionTEG AnalogROTEM AssayROTEM Parameter(s)
Intrinsic pathway / global factor activityR / CK-RINTEMCT (clotting time), CFT (clot formation time)
Extrinsic pathway, rapid readoutCRTEXTEMCT, CFT, A10 (amplitude at 10 min — an early surrogate for MCF)
Heparin effect isolationCKH vs. CKHEPTEM vs. INTEMCompare CT between the two assays
Isolated fibrinogen contributionCFFFIBTEMMCF or A10 (platelets blocked with cytochalasin D instead of a GPIIb/IIIa antagonist)
Overall clot strengthMAEXTEM / INTEMMCF (maximum clot firmness), or A10 as an early surrogate
HyperfibrinolysisLY30EXTEM ± APTEMML / LI30 (lysis index); APTEM adds aprotinin to confirm true hyperfibrinolysis

APTEM is the one assay with no direct TEG analog: it re-runs EXTEM with an antifibrinolytic added to the sample. If APTEM normalizes an abnormal EXTEM tracing, that confirms the abnormality was hyperfibrinolysis rather than a factor or platelet defect, supporting a decision to give TXA.

Goal-Directed Transfusion Framework

Applies to either result model — treat the deficiency the abnormal parameter names

If This Parameter Is Abnormal......The Deficiency Is......Treat With
R / CK-R prolonged (heparin excluded)Coagulation factorsFFP, or PCC if rapid correction is needed
CK-R prolonged but CKH-R normalHeparin effect, not factor deficiencyProtamine (patient-specific decision) rather than more factor product
K prolonged / Alpha angle low / CFF-MA lowFibrinogenCryoprecipitate, or fibrinogen concentrate where available
MA / CRT-MA / CK-based MA low (fibrinogen adequate)Platelet number or functionPlatelet transfusion; DDAVP for qualitative defects
LY30 / CK-LY30 elevatedHyperfibrinolysisTXA or aminocaproic acid — not more factor or platelet product
Treat the parameter that is actually abnormal. TEG's entire value is replacing empiric 1:1:1 resuscitation with a product chosen to match the specific defect on the tracing.
Platelets or fibrinogen? Read the two MAs together CRT MA measures platelets and fibrinogen combined; CFF MA measures fibrinogen alone. Low CRT MA with a normal CFF MA: fibrinogen is adequate, so the weak clot is a platelet problem. Low CRT MA with a low CFF MA: fibrinogen is contributing; replace fibrinogen first, then reassess before adding platelets.10
Worked example (07-604-US) CK R 14.2 min (normal 4.6–9.1) · CKH R 13.4 min · CK MA, CRTH MA, CFFH MA and LY30 all normal. R is prolonged on both channels, and adding heparinase barely shortens it. This is not heparin. It is a coagulation factor deficiency: plasma, or PCC if rapid correction is needed. Normal MAs mean platelets and fibrinogen don't need replacing.10
The other direction: hypercoagulability A short R and a high CFF or CRT MA suggest a hypercoagulable state. The device chart lists LY30 as not applicable here, but trauma literature describes a separate low-LY30 phenotype, fibrinolysis shutdown (LY30 below about 0.9%), associated with worse outcomes after injury. It is a research-defined category, not a device alert.11

Pitfalls & Pre-Analytical Limitations

The numbers are only as good as the sample and the technique behind them

PitfallEffectPractical Fix
Line/catheter contaminationA heparin flush contaminating the draw falsely prolongs RDiscard the first several mL when drawing through an indwelling line, or obtain a discrete venipuncture
Sample run outside the validated time windowCitrate rest-time drift changes R and KFollow the lab's rest-then-run protocol (commonly a ~30 min citrate rest before activation)
Testing at 37°C in a hypothermic patientStandard warming to 37°C can mask coagulopathy that is actually present at the patient's real temperatureConsider running at patient temperature in significant hypothermia where the platform supports it
Anemia / low hematocritParadoxically inflates MA and steepens the alpha angle, making the tracing look hypercoagulable independent of true platelet or fibrinogen status7Interpret MA in the context of the concurrent hematocrit, especially in massively transfused or hemodiluted patients
Thrombocytopenia and platelet dysfunction coexistingStandard MA cannot distinguish "too few platelets" from "platelets present but not working"Correlate with the platelet count directly, or add Platelet Mapping when qualitative dysfunction is suspected

Because pre-analytical variables move the numbers as much as real pathology does, trend the tracing against the clinical picture rather than treating an isolated abnormal value in a vacuum.2

Special Populations

Non-pregnant, non-cirrhotic reference ranges do not always apply

Pregnancy. Normal pregnancy is a physiologic hypercoagulable state — a shorter R, steeper alpha angle, and higher MA than non-pregnant reference ranges, driven by rising factor and fibrinogen levels and reduced fibrinolytic activity. In postpartum hemorrhage, fibrinogen is the first factor to fall to a critical level and the strongest single predictor of progression to severe hemorrhage, which is why obstetric protocols increasingly trend fibrinogen (via CFF-MA or FIBTEM) rather than waiting on standard coags.9

Cirrhosis / chronic liver disease. Conventional coagulation tests (INR, platelet count) overstate bleeding risk in cirrhosis because they only capture the loss of pro-coagulant factors, ignoring the parallel loss of anticoagulant proteins (protein C, protein S) and elevated von Willebrand factor that "rebalance" hemostasis at a lower overall set point.8 TEG/ROTEM better reflect this rebalanced state and have been used to reduce unnecessary pre-procedure FFP transfusion in cirrhotic patients — but neither test reliably captures the qualitative platelet defects or portal-hypertension-driven hyperfibrinolysis also common in advanced liver disease, so clinical correlation still matters.

Pediatric and neonatal. Developmental hemostasis means normal ranges shift meaningfully with age. Apply age-specific pediatric reference ranges rather than the adult cutoffs presented in this guide.

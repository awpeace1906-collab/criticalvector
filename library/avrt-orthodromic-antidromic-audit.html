Orthodromic vs Antidromic AVRT: Not Every Pathway Shows a Delta Wave

An ECGPlus infographic on the AVRT circuit. The physiology and the circuit drawings are right. The substrate box ties AVRT to the WPW pattern, which leaves out concealed pathways, and the management box needs a caution for antidromic AVRT.

R. E. Suscitate & A. Systole · November 2026. Original post: @ecgplus1, “Orthodromic vs Antidromic AVRT: same circuit, opposite direction.” This page is an independent third-party evaluation, not affiliated with or endorsed by the original creator.

Verdict: mechanism, ECG features and proportions match ESC 2019 and ACC/AHA/HRS 2015. Correction: orthodromic AVRT often runs on a concealed pathway with a normal baseline ECG, so “WPW pattern” is not part of the definition. The caption’s warning about AV nodal blockers in pre-excited AF is correct and deserves more space than the infographic gives it.

Same loop, opposite direction

Schematic. Down the AV node and His–Purkinje gives a normal, narrow QRS; down the pathway activates the ventricle muscle-to-muscle and gives a wide, pre-excited QRS. A concealed pathway can only carry the dashed (retrograde) limb, so it supports orthodromic AVRT alone.

Infographic and caption check

Post saysVerdictNote
Orthodromic: down AV node, up the pathway; narrow, regular; retrograde P after QRS; no delta wave during tachycardiaVerifiedCorrect, and the circuit drawing matches. Narrow unless aberrant conduction widens it.
Antidromic: down the pathway, up the AV node or a second pathway; wide, fully pre-excited; mimics VTVerifiedCorrect. Multiple pathways are common in antidromic AVRT.
~90–95% orthodromic, ~5–10% antidromicVerifiedConsistent with ESC 2019 and ACC/AHA/HRS 2015.
Trigger: a premature beat blocks in one limb and conducts down the otherVerifiedThe standard re-entry mechanism.
Substrate: Bundle of Kent … WPW patternCorrectionTrue only for manifest pathways. Many patients with orthodromic AVRT have a concealed pathway that conducts only retrograde, a normal resting ECG, and no WPW pattern. “Bundle of Kent” is the eponym; guidelines now say atrioventricular accessory pathway.
Management box: vagal / adenosine block the AV nodeNeeds contextRight for orthodromic AVRT (vagal maneuvers first, then adenosine). For antidromic AVRT adenosine can terminate the circuit, since the AV node is the retrograde limb, but it can provoke atrial fibrillation that then conducts down the pathway. Have a defibrillator attached.
Caption: antidromic — be careful with AV nodal blockers if pre-excited AF is possibleVerifiedThe right instinct. In pre-excited AF, avoid adenosine, beta blockers, calcium channel blockers, digoxin and (ESC 2019) IV amiodarone; cardiovert if unstable, procainamide or ibutilide if stable.
Unstable: synchronized cardioversion; catheter ablation is curativeVerifiedBoth class I in current guidelines.
Pearl: wide regular tachycardia in WPW, think antidromic, not just VTNeeds contextKeep the converse. Without a known pathway or a prior pre-excited ECG, a wide regular tachycardia is VT until proven otherwise.
03

In the resus bay

1

Unstable at any width

Synchronized cardioversion. This does not depend on working out which limb is antegrade.

2

Stable, narrow, regular

Modified Valsalva, then adenosine with a recording strip running. Termination with a retrograde P after the last QRS favors AVRT over AVNRT.

3

Stable, wide, regular, known WPW

Antidromic AVRT is likely but VT is still possible. Procainamide is the drug that treats both safely. Adenosine is reasonable only with pads on.

4

Wide, irregular, fast, varying QRS

Pre-excited AF. No AV nodal blockers. Procainamide or ibutilide if stable, cardioversion if not.

5

After the event

Electrophysiology referral. Ablation of the pathway is first-line, curative in most patients, and removes the sudden-death risk of a fast-conducting manifest pathway.

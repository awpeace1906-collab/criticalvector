SCAI SHOCK 2026: Perfusion Over Pressure

Critical Care Reviews posted the summary figure from the third SCAI SHOCK update. Stages, phenotypes and the arrest modifier match. The deterioration pathway uses “i.e.” where it means “e.g.”, and the core dataset is broader than the public draft.

R. E. Suscitate & A. Systole · November 2026. Original post: @criticalcarereviews, CCR Journal Watch. This page is an independent third-party evaluation, not affiliated with or endorsed by the original creator.

Verdict: the figure reflects the 2026 SCAI SHOCK update (JSCAI, November 2026). The largest practical change is that normotensive hypoperfusion is now stage C. The numeric thresholds on this page come from the June 2026 public-comment draft, because the final text was not yet accessible; confirm before teaching.

01

The stages, as refined

StageDefinitionNumeric anchors (public-comment draft)
A · At riskNo signs of shock but at risk. Normal pressure, lactate and mentation. Now reserved for patients with neither instability nor hypoperfusion.SBP ≥100; CI ≥2.5; CVP <10; PCWP <15
B · BeginningHemodynamic instability (relative hypotension or tachycardia) without hypoperfusionSBP <90 or MAP <60 or >30 drop; HR ≥100; lactate normal
C · ClassicHypoperfusion needing one intervention beyond volume. Normotensive hypoperfusion is C.Lactate ≥2; UO <30 mL/h; CI <2.2; PCWP >15
D · DeterioratingLike C, but worsening or not improving despite initial supportReassess by 2–3 h for worsening, 4–6 h for failure to improve
E · ExtremisActual or impending circulatory collapse. Defined by physiology, not by whether ECLS is running.Lactate ≥8; pH <7.2; base deficit >10

Thresholds taken from the June 2026 public-comment draft; confirm against the final JSCAI publication before teaching them.

03

What actually changed since 2022

C, not B
Normotensive hypoperfusion
A normal blood pressure no longer keeps a hypoperfused patient out of shock.
2–3 h
Reassessment interval
Serial lactate and exam to separate C from D; trajectory over time carries more prognosis than one snapshot.
7
Named phenotypes
Including post-cardiotomy and pediatric, each with its own guidance.

The update’s central message, in the chairs’ words, is to look past blood pressure to perfusion. Everything in the table above follows from that.

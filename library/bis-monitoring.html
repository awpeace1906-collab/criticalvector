01

What BIS Measures

The Bispectral Index processes raw frontal EEG into a single dimensionless number, 0 (isoelectric/no detectable activity) to 100 (fully awake), intended as a proxy for hypnotic depth of anesthesia.

What it is: a proprietary algorithm combining time-domain, frequency-domain, and higher-order spectral (bispectral) EEG analysis into one trended number.

What it is not: a measure of analgesia, a measure of paralysis, or a direct measure of "consciousness" — it is specifically a hypnotic-depth correlate, and a correlate only.

Value Ranges & Waveform Patterns

BIS rangeStateEEG character
90–100AwakeHigh frequency, low amplitude, random — inadequate anesthesia if this appears intraoperatively
70–89Light anesthesia / sedationBeta activity decreasing, alpha activity emerging
40–60Adequate general anesthesia (target range)Alpha activity predominant, <12 Hz
20–39Deep anesthesiaDelta activity dominant, 1–4 Hz
0–19Very deep / burst suppressionPeriods of EEG suppression (flat segments) interspersed with bursts of activity
0Isoelectric / flatlineNo detectable cortical activity — excessive anesthesia, cardiac arrest, or severe hypothermia

Target BIS for general anesthesia: 40–60. This range is associated with a low likelihood of explicit recall while avoiding the unnecessary depth (and hemodynamic cost) of burst suppression.

BIS is a trend monitor. A single number in isolation is far less useful than watching where it's heading and how it responds to a dose change — treat it the way you'd treat a single blood pressure reading versus a trend.

03

The Evidence: Does It Actually Work?

This is the section most BIS reference material skips, and it's the most clinically important one — the trial evidence on whether BIS monitoring actually reduces awareness is genuinely mixed, not settled.

TrialDesignFinding
B-Aware (Myles et al., Lancet 2004)2,463 high-risk patients, BIS-guided vs. routine careBIS-guided anesthesia reduced awareness incidence — the landmark positive trial. Number needed to treat to prevent one case of awareness: 138.
B-Unaware (Avidan et al., NEJM 2008)1,941 high-risk patients, BIS-guided (target 40-60) vs. end-tidal anesthetic gas (ETAG)-guided (target MAC 0.7-1.3) No significant difference in awareness between BIS-guided and ETAG-guided protocols — directly contradicted the premise that BIS specifically (rather than any structured protocol) was the active ingredient.
BAG-RECALL (Avidan et al., NEJM 2011)Prevention of intraoperative awareness in a high-risk surgical populationAgain found no significant awareness reduction from BIS-guided protocols compared to ETAG-guided protocols.

Why the trials disagree: a commonly raised critique of B-Aware is that its "routine care" comparison arm lacked a structured alternative protocol — meaning some of BIS's apparent benefit may reflect the effect of following any defined depth-monitoring protocol (increased vigilance) rather than something specific to the EEG-derived number itself. B-Unaware and BAG-RECALL, by giving the comparison arm an equally structured ETAG-based protocol, found the BIS-specific advantage disappeared.

The honest summary: BIS monitoring is widely used and reasonable, especially in high-risk-for-awareness cases or when volatile agent use is being minimized (TIVA, where end-tidal gas monitoring isn't available as a cross-check) — but "BIS prevents awareness better than watching your anesthetic gas concentration" is not a settled claim, it's a disputed one across three major trials.

04

Artifacts & Limitations

Source of errorEffect
EMG activity (shivering, muscle tone, patient movement)Falsely elevates BIS — can look like inadequate anesthesia when the patient is actually adequately anesthetized but under-paralyzed
Electrocautery interferenceFalsely elevates BIS during active cautery use
Poor electrode contact / loose sensorUnreliable or falsely elevated reading — always check signal quality index before trusting the number
KetamineCan paradoxically increase BIS despite clinically adequate or even deep anesthesia — a genuine paradox, since ketamine's dissociative EEG pattern doesn't map onto the BIS algorithm the way propofol/volatile-driven EEG changes do
HypothermiaDecreases BIS independent of anesthetic depth

Before trusting any BIS value: confirm electrode contact, check the signal quality index (>50%), rule out cautery/EMG interference, and correlate with hemodynamics and clinical signs — a BIS number that doesn't match the clinical picture is a reason to distrust the monitor, not the patient.

BIS does not detect analgesia and does not detect paralysis — a patient can be fully paralyzed, inadequately anesthetized, and in significant physiologic distress from nociception with a BIS reading that looks reassuring. It is one input among several, not a replacement for clinical judgment.

BIS is a useful trend monitor with real limitations and a genuinely contested evidence base for its headline claim — awareness prevention. Use it as one input among several, correlate with clinical signs, and don't treat the number as more certain than the trials behind it are.

Hypoxemia vs Hypoxia

R. E. Suscitate & A. Systole

One is a blood gas number. The other is a cell running out of oxygen. Five mechanisms produce the first, two bedside questions separate them, and the answer decides whether more oxygen will help.

VERIFIEDSarkar et al., Lung India 2017Corrects a circulating caption
Hypoxemia
in the blood · measured

Low oxygen in arterial blood: a low PaO₂ on the gas, or a low SpO₂ as its surrogate.

A lung and gas-exchange problem until shown otherwise.

Hypoxia
in the tissue · inferred

Oxygen delivery or use that falls short of tissue demand. Shows up as lactate, organ dysfunction, and altered mental status.

Can exist with a perfect saturation.

On the source caption. "Dead space = oxygenates, shunt = doesn't" swaps the terms. The mechanism that responds to supplemental oxygen is low V/Q (perfusion exceeding ventilation). Dead space is the opposite end, high V/Q, and mainly costs CO₂ clearance rather than oxygenation. The shunt half is right.

01

Hypoxia without hypoxemia

Oxygen delivery = cardiac output × arterial oxygen content. PaO₂ is only one input.

TypeWhat failsSpO₂Examples
HypoxemicArterial oxygenationLowPneumonia, ARDS, PE, hypoventilation

A normal saturation does not clear a patient of hypoxia. A lactate and a look at perfusion are the check.

Where dead space fits. Dead space is ventilation without perfusion (V/Q → ∞). It raises the work of breathing and the PaCO₂. When a PE drops the saturation, the culprit is mostly overperfusion of the remaining lung (low V/Q) and shunt, not the dead space itself.

03

A-a gradient

PAO₂ = FiO₂ × (Patm − 47) − PaCO₂ / 0.8. Gradient = PAO₂ − PaO₂. The expected value on room air is roughly (age ÷ 4) + 4 mmHg, and it widens with higher FiO₂, so it is most useful on room air.

04

Bedside approach

1
Confirm the number. Check the waveform and probe site; a poor pleth signal or cold extremity can read falsely low. If carbon monoxide or methemoglobin is possible, get co-oximetry.
2
Look at the PaCO₂. High PaCO₂ with a normal A-a gradient is pure hypoventilation. Treat the ventilation, not just the saturation.
3
Calculate the A-a gradient. Normal: low inspired O₂ or hypoventilation. Raised: the lung is involved.
4
Watch the response to oxygen. A brisk rise favors V/Q mismatch or diffusion. Little rise despite high FiO₂ means shunt, and shunt needs alveoli opened, not more oxygen.

Pitfall: oxygen hides hypoventilation. On supplemental oxygen a patient can hold a normal SpO₂ while PaCO₂ climbs. A reassuring saturation in a drowsy patient after opioids or sedation is not proof of adequate ventilation; check CO₂.

05

Fix by mechanism

Hypoventilation

Reverse the cause (naloxone for opioids), then support ventilation with NIV or intubation. Oxygen alone buys saturation, not CO₂ clearance.

V/Q mismatch

Supplemental oxygen works. Treat the driver: bronchodilators, antibiotics, anticoagulation.

Shunt

Recruit alveoli: PEEP or CPAP, positioning, prone positioning in ARDS. Drain, diurese, or re-expand what is filling or collapsing them.

Hypoxia with a normal SpO₂

Restore delivery: blood for anemia, flow for shock, and specific antidotes for CO (high-flow oxygen) or cyanide (hydroxocobalamin).

Contraindications to Neuraxial

Absolute

  • Patient refusal
  • Coagulopathy: INR >1.5 · Plt <80×10⁹/L (ASRA 2023)
  • Active therapeutic anticoagulation — see ASRA intervals below
  • Skin infection at injection site
  • Elevated ICP / mass lesion (herniation risk)
  • Lack of resuscitation equipment or monitoring

Relative

  • Hypovolemia — stabilize first
  • Pre-existing neurologic disease — document baseline
  • Severe AS/MS (↓ SVR → ↓ CO)
  • Systemic infection (bacteremia) — individualize risk/benefit
  • Indeterminate surgical duration

Spinal Hypotension

Prevention
Co-load 500–1000 mL crystalloid at block placement (not pre-load). Preemptive phenylephrine infusion 25–50 mcg/min OR norepinephrine 0.05–0.1 mcg/kg/min started at block. Ondansetron 4 mg IV reduces vasovagal component.

Treatment

  • Phenylephrine 50–200 mcg IV bolus → infusion 25–100 mcg/min
  • Norepinephrine 5–10 mcg IV bolus → infusion 0.05–0.1 mcg/kg/min — OB preferred (↓ reflex brady vs phenylephrine)
  • Ephedrine 5–10 mg IV — prefer if bradycardia + hypotension coexist
  • Atropine 0.5 mg IV for isolated bradycardia; low threshold for epi
  • IV fluid bolus if hypovolemic component
  • LUD if parturient
2024 OB Update
Norepinephrine now equivalent or superior to phenylephrine for OB spinal hypotension — better HR maintenance, similar neonatal outcomes. Consider as first-line where available.

🚨 LOCAL ANESTHETIC SYSTEMIC TOXICITY — ASRA 2023
Delayed onset up to 60 min post-injection. Maintain vigilance. Stock Intralipid wherever regional anesthesia is performed.
⚡

CNS Signs

  • Perioral / tongue numbness
  • Metallic taste, tinnitus
  • Lightheadedness, visual Δ
  • Agitation, confusion, slurred speech
  • Muscle twitching → seizures
  • Coma, respiratory arrest
💓

CV Signs

  • Hypertension (early)
  • QRS widening, PR prolongation
  • Bradycardia, heart block
  • Hypotension, ventricular arrhythmias
  • VT/VF — resistant to std ACLS
  • Cardiac arrest
Bupivacaine Warning
CV collapse may be FIRST sign — minimal CNS prodrome. Delayed onset >5 min reported with large depot injections. Ultrasound guidance reduces but does NOT eliminate LAST risk.
🚑

LAST Treatment Algorithm — ASRA 2023

1
Stop Injection + Call for Help
Immediately stop LA. Announce LAST. Activate code team. Notify nearest ECMO center early if arrest seems likely.
2
Airway — 100% O₂
Maintain patent airway. 100% O₂ via NRB or BVM. Intubate if necessary. Hypoxia + acidosis ↑↑ cardiotoxicity. Avoid propofol for intubation if hemodynamically compromised.
3
Seizure Control — Benzos First
Benzodiazepines preferred. Small propofol doses acceptable if hemodynamically stable only.
Midazolam: 1–5 mg IV (titrate)
Lorazepam: 2–4 mg IV
Propofol: 0.5–1 mg/kg IV — only if CV stable
Phenobarbital: 15–20 mg/kg IV if refractory
4
Intralipid 20% — Start Immediately
Do NOT delay for cardiac arrest. Use ideal body weight. Max cumulative ~12 mL/kg.
Bolus: 1.5 mL/kg IBW IV over 2–3 min
Infusion: 0.25 mL/kg/min (↑ to 0.5 mL/kg/min if refractory)
Repeat bolus × 1–2 for refractory arrest
70 kg example: ~105 mL bolus → ~18 mL/min infusion
Max cumulative: ≤12 mL/kg (≈ 840 mL for 70 kg)
5
Cardiac Arrest — Modified ACLS
Key deviations from standard ACLS:
✅ Epinephrine: ≤1 mcg/kg IV boluses (↓ dose — large doses worsen VF + impair lipid resuscitation)
✅ Amiodarone 150 mg IV for refractory VT/VF
✅ Continue Intralipid throughout CPR
✅ Prolonged CPR — 60–90 min; recovery reported
❌ Avoid vasopressin — worsens outcomes in LAST
❌ Avoid Ca-channel blockers / beta-blockers
❌ Avoid lidocaine as antiarrhythmic
6
ECMO / Cardiopulmonary Bypass
Refractory arrest despite optimal resuscitation + LET → activate ECMO team. Best outcomes with early institution. ECMO provides circulatory support while LA redistributes.
7
Post-Event Monitoring ≥ 2 hr
Delayed recurrence reported. ICU admission recommended after severe LAST. Intralipid may interfere with: triglycerides, lipase, LFTs, some drug assays.
📋

LAST Prevention

  • Use ultrasound guidance — ↓ incidence but does NOT eliminate risk
  • Fractionated injection: 3–5 mL increments with 30–60 sec pauses
  • Epinephrine test dose each increment: ↑HR >10 bpm = intravascular
  • Aspirate before each injection
  • Use minimum effective dose and concentration
  • Prefer ropivacaine or levobupivacaine over bupivacaine for large-volume blocks
  • Stock Intralipid 20% wherever blocks are performed
  • High-risk patients: extremes of age, cardiac/hepatic disease, low protein, pregnancy
Intralipid Stocking (ASRA 2023)
20% Intralipid must be immediately available (≤60 sec) wherever large-volume regional anesthesia performed. Typical stocking: 4–6 × 250 mL bags or equivalent. IO administration acceptable if no IV access.
Regional Anesthesia — General Principles
Use ultrasound guidance (standard of care). Fractionated injection. Epinephrine test dose. Have Intralipid stocked. Document pre-block neuro exam. New CPT codes effective Jan 1, 2025 for thoracic + lower extremity fascial plane blocks.

UPPER EXTREMITY

Interscalene Block
C5–C6 (shoulder/prox humerus) · US-guided
▼

Coverage

  • Shoulder surgery, proximal humerus, AC joint
  • C5–C6 (C7 variable)
  • Spares: ulnar nerve (C8–T1), medial arm

Dosing

Volume15–20 mL
Bupivacaine 0.5%15–20 mL
Ropivacaine 0.5%15–20 mL
Continuous0.2% rop 5–8 mL/hr

Complications

  • Phrenic nerve block (100%) → ↓ ipsilateral diaphragm — avoid in severe contralateral lung disease
  • Pneumothorax (<1% US-guided)
  • Horner syndrome (ptosis, miosis, anhidrosis)
  • Recurrent laryngeal nerve block → hoarseness
  • Vertebral artery injection (rare)

Notes

US: probe at neck; identify SCM, scalene muscles, C5/C6 roots between ASM/MSM. Low-volume (10 mL) reduces phrenic palsy. Superficial injection target.

Supraclavicular Block
C5–T1 (entire upper extremity below shoulder) · US-guided
▼

Coverage

  • Elbow, forearm, wrist, hand surgery
  • All trunks of brachial plexus at compact point

Dosing

Volume20–35 mL
Bupivacaine 0.375–0.5%20–30 mL
Ropivacaine 0.5%20–30 mL

Axillary Block
Musculocutaneous + terminal branches · Safest brachial approach
▼

Coverage

  • Multiple injections required

Thoracic + abdominal analgesia · Versatile · Emerging evidence
▼

Mechanism

Dosing

ASA/APSF/ESAIC 2023 — NMB Monitoring Standard
Quantitative neuromuscular monitoring at adductor pollicis (ulnar nerve stimulation) is the ONLY reliable method to confirm TOF ratio ≥0.9 before extubation. Clinical tests (head lift, hand grip, tongue depressor) do NOT reliably detect residual block. Residual NMB rate: 30–65% without quantitative monitoring.

High / Total Spinal

  • Rapid unexpected cephalad spread of block
  • Numbness / paresthesia / weakness of upper extremities
  • SOB, apnea, anxiety, nausea
  • Bradycardia ± hypotension
  • Loss of consciousness → cardiac arrest (total spinal)

Treatment

  • Call for help, code cart, inform surgical team immediately
  • If cardiac arrest: CPR + ACLS protocol
  • Support ventilation — intubate if apneic
  • Epi 10 mcg IV boluses for bradycardia/hypotension; titrate up
  • Atropine 0.5 mg IV for mild bradycardia; low threshold for epi
  • IV fluid bolus wide open
IF PARTURIENT
LUD immediately. Alert OB team. Prepare for perimortem C/S. Monitor fetal HR. If arrest → ACLS in Pregnancy protocol.

Malignant Hyperthermia (MH)

Trigger Agents

  • All volatile agents (sevo, iso, des, halothane)
  • Succinylcholine
  • SAFE: propofol, ketamine, opioids, benzos, NdNMBs, N₂O, local anesthetics

Early Signs

  • ↑ EtCO₂ (first reliable sign)
  • Masseter spasm / generalized rigidity
  • ↑ HR, ↑ RR, hyperthermia (>1°C/5 min)
  • Metabolic acidosis, ↑ K⁺, myoglobinuria

Treatment — MHAUS Hotline: 1-800-644-9737

1
Dantrolene — Immediately
2.5 mg/kg IV rapid bolus → repeat 2.5 mg/kg q5 min PRN (MHAUS/EMHG 2024: repeat full 2.5 mg/kg dose, not 1 mg/kg); max 10 mg/kg (up to 20 mg/kg if refractory). Maintenance post-crisis: 1 mg/kg IV q4–6h × ≥24h to prevent recrudescence (occurs in ~20% of cases, median 9h after crisis). Ryanodex 250 mg/5 mL DW (faster reconstitution than standard 20 mg/vial).
2
Discontinue All Triggers
Stop volatile agents. Switch to TIVA (propofol ± opioid). Hyperventilate 100% O₂ ≥10 L/min. Change circuit and soda lime when time permits.
3
Active Cooling
Ice to groin/axilla/neck. Cold IVF lavage. Cool body cavities if open. Stop cooling at 38.5°C (overshoot risk).
4
Treat Metabolic Consequences
Bicarb for acidosis. CaCl for hyperkalemia. Insulin + D50 for K⁺. Amiodarone for arrhythmias. UO >1 mL/kg/hr; alkalinize urine for rhabdomyolysis.
5
ICU — ≥24h Post-Crisis
Dantrolene 1 mg/kg IV q4–6h × ≥24h (MHAUS: bolus preferred over infusion; allows clinical reassessment at each interval). Recrudescence in ~20% — monitor for return of rigidity, ↑EtCO₂, ↑CK. Monitor: CK, BMP, UA q6h. CK peaks 12–24h. Oral dantrolene 4–8 mg/kg/day ÷ 4 doses × 1–3 days if transitioning off IV. Refer patient + family for RYR1 genetic testing (CHCT or DNA).

Anaphylaxis

Common triggers: Antibiotics · NMBDs (roc, sux) · Latex · Chlorhexidine · Contrast · Colloids · Opioids

1
Epinephrine — First and Only First Line
Arrest: 0.5–1 mg IV → ACLS. Hypotension/bronchospasm: 10–50 mcg IV q1–2 min. No IV: 300–500 mcg IM (anterolateral thigh). Infusion: 0.05–1 mcg/kg/min
2
Airway + 100% O₂
Intubate early if airway edema developing. Albuterol 2.5 mg neb for bronchospasm.
3
Volume + Vasopressors
1–2 L crystalloid IV rapidly. Vasopressin 0.03–0.04 units/min for epi-refractory hypotension. Norepinephrine infusion if needed.
4
Adjuncts (Secondary)
Diphenhydramine 25–50 mg IV · Famotidine 20 mg IV (H₂; ranitidine RECALLED — NDMA) · Methylprednisolone 125 mg IV · Glucagon 1–5 mg IV if beta-blocked
Rocuronium/Vecuronium Anaphylaxis
Sugammadex 16 mg/kg IV — encapsulates the antigenic NMBD, removes ongoing stimulus. Does NOT treat the anaphylaxis itself — still requires full anaphylaxis treatment.

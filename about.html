About This Site

The The Critical Vector is a blog written by a physician-in-training with a background in prehospital and critical care medicine. The writing sits at the intersection of emergency medicine, anesthesiology, and critical care — the places where those disciplines aren't separate fields but sequential phases of the same patient encounter.

The name comes from the idea of direction and magnitude. A vector has both. In medicine, knowing what is wrong matters less than knowing where to move and how fast. That tension — between diagnosis and action, between protocol and judgment — is what most of these posts are actually about.

"The purpose of human life is to serve, and to show compassion and the will to help others." — Albert Schweitzer

The site is organized around four sections:

The Continuum traces patient care across the handoff points — prehospital, emergency department, ICU, operating room. Cases and commentary that don't fit neatly into one specialty because real patients don't either.

The Workshop is where the tools live. Clinical reference guides, offline-ready HTML resources, and procedural education materials built for use at the bedside or in the field.

The Evidence Locker is for research — literature breakdowns, case reports, and the kind of papers worth actually reading rather than just citing.

The Stack is a curated list of external resources. The sites, podcasts, and databases that actually get used.

Nothing here constitutes medical advice. Everything here is written by someone still in training, thinking out loud about medicine they find genuinely interesting. Read accordingly.
